## Facility definition

OWS-006 is a pilot-scale agricultural microbiology laboratory for controlled comparison of fungal cultures with and without the PT-9 bacterial symbiont. It is not a clinic, a generic wet lab or a miniature production farm. Its architecture must support repeatable culture trials, sample custody, controlled environments, observation, material compatibility testing and secure reporting.

The intact D0 facility must read as four connected systems:

1. **public/research threshold** — visitor reception, VCF product interpretation and badge-controlled staff entry;
2. **sample and preparation chain** — receiving, accession, clean stock, media/reagent preparation and culture inoculation;
3. **comparative trial suite** — three monitored chambers with operator observation and distinct experimental roles;
4. **analysis and custody chain** — bacterial controls, polymer coupon work, imaging/data review and secure records.

## Primary experimental workflow

The operating sequence is:

`controlled sample/reagent receiving -> accession and cold/clean hold -> media/reagent preparation -> inoculation -> chamber assignment -> monitored growth -> controlled sampling -> bacterial comparison and polymer-coupon analysis -> data review -> secure records/reporting`

The three principal chambers should have legible roles:

- **Chamber A — EP-7 reference:** fungus without PT-9, establishing the control response;
- **Chamber B — EP-7 + PT-9 symbiosis:** the successful protected culture and principal VCF showcase;
- **Chamber C — stress/recovery trial:** interrupted nutrient or environmental conditions demonstrating why the paired system appeared robust.

The polymer observation suite is a linked analysis branch, not a fourth decorative culture room. It receives controlled PT-9 samples and material coupons, compares degradation, retains failed seals/membranes and sends results to secure records.

## Containment and clean/dirty logic

The site belongs to an early-anomaly period: VCF does not yet understand the global consequence, but a professional microbiology laboratory still separates clean supplies from active cultures and waste.

Required intact boundaries are:

- public reception separated from staff research circulation;
- staff change/handwash threshold before controlled laboratory access;
- sample receipt accessible from service receiving without crossing the lobby;
- preparation upstream of active culture rooms;
- chamber vestibules/pass-throughs that avoid opening culture interiors directly into a public corridor;
- observation access that permits monitoring without entering each chamber;
- decontamination/wash and waste hold on a service route;
- independent or branchable air/service connections for the polymer suite and each chamber;
- secure records beyond general public access but reachable through a safe player route.

## People and material circulation

Distinguishable flows:

- **visitor:** forecourt -> reception/exhibit -> glazed observation gallery -> exit;
- **staff:** badge vestibule -> lockers/handwash -> preparation/control corridor -> chambers/analysis -> offices/records;
- **sample/reagent:** service receiving -> accession/cold hold -> preparation -> controlled pass-through -> chamber/analysis;
- **waste/used material:** chamber/analysis -> wash/decon -> secure waste hold -> rear service removal;
- **maintenance:** service entrance -> utility corridor -> chamber plant/roof plant without crossing the clean preparation chain.

## Hero space

The memorable intact space is a controlled observation gallery facing the three comparative chambers. The player should understand the experiment from chamber numbering, distinct chamber contents, control consoles, sample-transfer points and visible service connections before reading any lore.

The polymer suite is the narrative counterpoint: a smaller yellow-isolated room where degraded gaskets, membranes and coupons accumulate beside monitoring and secure records. It should initially read as a manageable materials-compatibility problem, not an evil containment chamber.
