# OWS-001 Visual Gate A — r1 Massing Review

## Review target

**Target:** OWS-001 — Verdant Continuum Foods Neighborhood Culture Service Depot  
**Gate:** Visual Gate A — D0 intact massing  
**Revision:** `massing-r1@9548586f`  
**Dimensions:** 39 x 13 x 33  
**Fixed camera set:** `ows001_fixed_v1`  
**Artifact manifest:** `old_world_narrative/reviews/heavy_rebuild/visual/OWS-001/gate_a_massing/r1/review_manifest.json`

The r1 set exists specifically to answer whether the new shell is worth developing. It is not judged on machinery, signs, damage, loot or microdetail.

## Baseline comparison

r1 is a real improvement over the Phase-0 grocery baseline in one essential respect: the building is no longer one donor shell with a VCF-colored overlay.

The new composition establishes separate masses for:

- a front public pavilion;
- a central workplace body;
- a lower west return/sanitation annex;
- a taller east clean cold-chain volume;
- a rear receiving/dispatch volume;
- a roof mechanical zone.

That is the correct direction and proves the 39 x 13 x 33 site can hold the program.

However, Gate A is not passed merely because the design is better than the old one.

## Front-left / front-right findings

### What works

- The public entrance now has its own projected pavilion rather than being merely a door in the grocery facade.
- The west and east operational masses create useful functional asymmetry.
- VCF green is reduced to an accent/identity blade rather than covering the entire building.
- The overall height remains believable for common neighborhood infrastructure.

### What fails

**1. The front canopy is too broad and too planar.**

The r1 canopy spans approximately X 9–29 and Z 1–5 as a nearly uninterrupted slab. It creates hierarchy, but it reads more like a large floating plate than a designed neighborhood-service canopy.

**Required r2 response:** narrow/segment the canopy, give it support rhythm, and let the entrance pavilion—not one giant slab—create the hierarchy.

**2. The central body is still visually over-dominant.**

The central X 8–31 / Z 8–28 body remains a large rectangular mass behind the new attachments. The annexes improve the outline, but the main block can still read as the old box wearing a more sophisticated set of additions.

**Required r2 response:** reduce uninterrupted central-wall/roof spans through setbacks, roof-step changes or more deliberate overlap between the public, return and cold-chain volumes.

## Rear-left / rear-right findings

### What works

- Rear service architecture is now materially different from the public facade.
- A dedicated receiving/dispatch volume is the correct massing move.
- Freight function can plausibly occupy the high-Z side without confusing public circulation.

### What fails

**3. The rear canopy is still diagrammatic.**

The rear service canopy is a broad rectangular slab rather than a canopy clearly tied to the actual freight opening.

**Required r2 response:** make the canopy narrower and align its width/depth to a specific loading threshold and staging point.

**4. Side/rear wall rhythm is too weak.**

The r1 shells are intentionally primitive, but long blank planes remain on the service volumes. Gate A does not require final fenestration, yet the massing should already imply structural/service bays.

**Required r2 response:** introduce a few major recesses, service-bay frames or material-depth changes that can later host real doors/windows/utilities.

## Roof/top findings

### What works

- The cold-chain function finally has visible rooftop support.
- Mechanical mass sits above/near the clean cold-chain side rather than being arbitrary generic HVAC.
- The roofline is no longer completely flat and anonymous.

### What fails

**5. Roof equipment is too solid and box-like.**

Two large sheet-metal prisms make the intended mechanical plant legible, but they are closer to rooftop bunkers than serviceable condenser/refrigeration equipment.

**Required r2 response:** break the plant into smaller repeated equipment masses, open service frames, narrow connecting runs and visible maintenance space. Preserve the overall plant location but reduce visual bulk.

**6. Mechanical screening is too abstract.**

The long linear screen elements currently read as marks on the roof rather than architecture serving the equipment.

**Required r2 response:** use partial screen walls/frames that wrap or organize the equipment without hiding it completely.

## Site-occupation findings

### What works

- Front and rear approaches exist as distinct site relationships.
- The footprint remains compact and plausibly neighborhood-scaled.

### What fails

**7. The site pad is too continuous.**

A nearly full-lot asphalt rectangle makes the site read as a diagram base rather than a real neighborhood property.

**Required r2 response:** retain a defined rear service apron and front arrival hardscape, but break the remaining perimeter into smaller pavement/utility/landscape strips so the building has a believable edge condition.

## Interior/cutaway capacity findings

The r1 cutaway is not an interior design and is not scored as one. Its job is to prove the masses can hold future program.

The required public, cold-chain, return, and rear-service volumes are all physically possible within the retained 39 x 13 x 33 bounds.

One risk is visible in the overlapping-shell strategy: later structural/interior design must remove accidental overlap walls and avoid squeezing the central staff spine into leftover space. That becomes a Pass 7/8 responsibility and is not a Gate-A failure by itself.

## Dimension decision

**39 x 13 x 33 remains accepted for r2.**

No expansion is justified yet. The problems identified are composition problems, not lack-of-space problems.

## Gate-A r1 decision

**REVISION REQUIRED.**

r1 is a successful design experiment but not a Gate-A pass.

The following seven defects must be addressed before the project may advance into structural/interior passes:

1. over-broad flat public canopy;
2. central body still too dominant;
3. rear canopy insufficiently tied to loading geometry;
4. weak side/rear structural-service rhythm;
5. roof plant too solid/box-like;
6. mechanical screening too abstract;
7. site hardscape too continuous.

No machinery, detailed interiors, damage, rubble, loot dressing or microdetail should be added while these macro defects remain.
