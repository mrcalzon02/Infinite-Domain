# [SYSTEM REPORT] OWS-001 — Pass 18 Damage and Decay

## Principle

D3 damage must be the physical result of **time, water, failed maintenance and abandonment acting on the approved D0 building**. It may not become a random ruin generator.

## D1 anomaly overlay

D1 modifies operations before it modifies structure.

- Add a small, localized inspection/hold marking at the west quality-control side.
- Increase suspect returned-container/replacement-seal stock near sanitation and quality hold.
- Mark one cold-service interface for repeated maintenance rather than disabling the entire cold chain.
- Add limited batch-exception evidence at receiving/check rather than converting the depot into emergency command space.
- Preserve public culture issue and most normal operations.

## D3 centuries-later structural decay

### Roof/service failure

The most important structural cause is long-term water ingress around the roof cold-plant/service zone after maintenance ceases. One localized roof/service-joint failure should damage the interior below and interrupt the refrigeration feed. Avoid evenly distributed roof holes.

### East cold-chain damage

One cooler bank/service segment may be physically failed or partially inaccessible, but enough repeated equipment remains to identify the original cold-storage system. The three-block service aisle must remain reconstructable and navigable.

### West return/sanitation damage

Water/drain/service penetrations make the west annex a plausible secondary decay focus. Use localized broken wall/ceiling material, damp/mossy masonry and displaced return containers rather than total annex erasure.

### Rear receiving exposure

The large freight opening is a believable centuries-long weather entry point. Gravel, displaced pallets/crates and localized floor deterioration belong around the loading edge, while the staff spine and route to records remain open.

### Public frontage

The front pavilion should remain the most recognizable portion of the ruin. Some glazing may be missing and fascia/signage may be incomplete, but `VERDANT CONTINUUM FOODS` / facility identity must remain inferable from surviving signage and color/architecture.

## Material language

Preferred decay substitutions are causal and restrained:

- `minecraft:cracked_stone_bricks`
- `minecraft:mossy_stone_bricks`
- localized `minecraft:gravel`
- localized `minecraft:coarse_dirt`
- limited missing glazing / air
- interrupted `create:fluid_pipe`
- displaced crates/pallets
- sparse cobweb/vegetation only where enclosure failure supports it

Do not blanket-convert clean white/green VCF surfaces into brown rubble. The contrast between surviving institutional architecture and decay is part of the storytelling.

## Protected invariants in D3

The final damage pass must preserve:

- identifiable public entrance;
- the distinct return and issue handoff geometry;
- the central staff spine as the primary exploration axis;
- readable west-return vs east-clean zoning;
- at least one clear route from entrance to supervisor/records proof;
- enough roof/cold hardware to explain the refrigeration system;
- enough signage to name VCF and the facility;
- deterministic proof acquisition.

## Gate-C requirement

Gate C must render **D0, D1 and D3 from the same fixed camera set**. D1 must be visibly but modestly different from D0. D3 must show centuries of causal decay while preserving the D0 building's underlying logic.
