# [SYSTEM REPORT] OWS-004 Pass 7 — Structural System

**Target:** OWS-004 — Verdant Continuum Foods Mycological Vertical Farm Tower  
**Status:** COMPLETE

## Structural intent

Gate-A massing established a stepped podium, four production modules, an attached service spine, integrated secondary egress mass and a greenhouse/environmental crown. Gate B must now make those masses behave like one coherent high-rise agricultural building.

## Primary frame

The main tower uses repeated structural bays aligned to the four cultivation modules rather than the donor's office-window cadence. Each production level requires:

- continuous floor plate capable of supporting dense grow banks and service equipment;
- perimeter columns/ribs aligned vertically through the repeated modules;
- stronger framing around the freight/environmental spine connection;
- clear floor openings only where vertical circulation or service transfer requires them;
- no decorative columns inside protected player/service aisles.

The lower two broader tower modules may retain more of the donor plate, while upper levels step inward. That setback must transfer through visible structural bands rather than floating upper walls.

## Podium structure

The public/demo, receiving/service and harvest/dispatch masses remain separate but structurally tied to the tower base. Canopies require visible supports. Large dock openings require complete frames rather than wall holes. Public glazing should sit within supported frames instead of replacing entire load-bearing walls.

## Service spine

The east-side spine is a structural/service tower in its own right. It must:

- connect to every production floor;
- continue to the crown/roof plant;
- contain protected service/freight volume;
- support risers and environmental branch points;
- remain externally distinguishable from the cultivation bays.

## Secondary egress/core

The opposite attached core replaces the baseline's dangling exterior stairs. It must provide a continuous protected vertical route with real landings at production levels and a roof-access relationship where feasible.

## Crown structure

The greenhouse crown requires a repeated frame supporting large glazed panels, not one unsupported glass cube. The environmental plant should sit on a defined service deck tied to the spine. Roof maintenance routes need a structural walking surface and guarded edges where appropriate.

## Containment-ready upper module

The upper cultivation floor's structural boundaries should already permit later isolation: a controlled core connection, robust perimeter/partition line and separable service branch. Later Gate C may add seals and damage, but not invent a new structural compartment.

## Gate-B structural checks

Reject the intact model if:

- cultivation floors float or have broken slab continuity;
- service-core openings interrupt required support without framing;
- vertical routes collide with columns/equipment masses;
- roof plant or crown lacks believable support;
- dock/canopy openings are unsupported;
- the upper containment-capable module has no physical boundary from lower floors.

**STRUCTURAL SYSTEM: COMPLETE.**
