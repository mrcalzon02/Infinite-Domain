## Real-world precedent study

OWS-001 is fictional, but its workflow should borrow from real facilities that separate public intake, controlled biological handling, inspection, cold storage, records, and service access.

### 1. Blood Bank of Alaska — Anchorage consolidated blood-bank facility

Reference: Neeser Construction project description, Blood Bank of Alaska.

Relevant precedent:

- Public donor functions coexist with processing/laboratory functions.
- Material receiving and vehicle/garage access are treated as separate operational functions rather than routed through the public area.
- Laboratory support, IT, administration, training, and staff spaces exist because the biological service requires more than its public counter.

Minecraft translation:

- Keep a clear public-facing issue/return threshold.
- Keep service receiving physically separate.
- Give the supervisor/records function an actual place rather than hiding evidence in generic storage.

### 2. Biobank Bern / Inselspital biobank infrastructure

Reference: Biobank Bern infrastructure description.

Relevant precedent:

- Reception and pre-analytical processing are integrated into a defined workflow.
- Automated cold storage is access-controlled and designed for uninterrupted operation.
- Samples move between processing and storage through dedicated infrastructure.
- Barcode/record tracking and controlled freezing are integral to the material flow.

Minecraft translation:

- Culture lockers form a dense controlled bank rather than a grocery cooler wall.
- Batch/temperature inspection belongs between receiving and clean cold holding.
- Staff circulation around the cold chain should be more direct than public circulation.
- Mechanical refrigeration support must be visible and plausible.

### 3. WHO temperature-controlled pharmaceutical warehouse guidance

Reference: WHO guidance on design of pharmaceutical warehouses and dispensing facilities.

Relevant precedent:

- Temperature-controlled storage, cold/freezer rooms, order assembly, packing, staging, loading docks, incoming stock, returns, separation of stock, and patient/public areas are distinct design concerns.

Minecraft translation:

- Receiving, cold holding, issue, return, and dispatch require separate zones even in a compact facility.
- Returned biological material must not share the same uncontrolled path as clean issue stock.
- Staging should sit near the service exit rather than in the middle of the public route.

### 4. National Biobank of Korea sample handling workflow

Reference: National Biobank of Korea biobanking process description.

Relevant precedent:

- Incoming material is inspected against records.
- Containers are checked for contamination and physical damage before storage.
- Quality control and traceable location records are part of ordinary operation.
- Material remains inside a controlled cold chain during transport and storage.

Minecraft translation:

- Receiving includes a batch/temperature check station.
- The early anomaly can be expressed as one returned batch moved into QUALITY HOLD after inspection rather than as a building-wide apocalypse.
- The proof manifest belongs in the records/supervisor node that governed this workflow.

## Functional program

### Clean product flow

`rear delivery -> receiving -> temperature/batch inspection -> clean refrigerated holding -> culture locker staging -> public issue counter`

### Return flow

`public return counter -> return intake -> sanitation/inspection -> quality decision -> reusable crate consolidation OR local quality hold -> rear service dispatch`

### Public route

`front vestibule -> information/queue -> culture issue or return counter -> exit`

The public route must not require passage through receiving, sanitation, quarantine, crate consolidation, or staff records.

### Staff route

`staff/service access -> supervisor/records -> receiving -> cold holding -> issue support -> sanitation/returns -> dispatch`

### Freight/service route

`rear approach -> receiving -> clean stock/return consolidation -> rear dispatch`

The three routes may intersect at controlled handoff points but should remain visually understandable.

## Historical layering

### Layer 1 — Normal operation

The depot is clean, successful, mundane, and heavily used. The main story is that culture distribution and return were normal neighborhood infrastructure.

### Layer 2 — Early anomaly

A recurring return/cold-chain quality problem appears. Staff isolate one bay or batch and continue normal service.

### Later layers

This particular target should not be over-written with late Firebreak or military history. Centuries of decay may be applied later, but enough of the operating depot must remain legible to reconstruct its original function.

## Environmental-story requirement

Without opening a book, the player should be able to infer:

- VCF cultures were distributed at neighborhood scale;
- customers received cultures through a controlled issue process;
- return containers/material came back through a separate route;
- returned material was washed/inspected for reuse;
- one return segment had begun failing quality checks;
- local workers attempted to isolate the problem while the rest of the depot kept operating.

The physical separation of clean lockers from return sanitation is therefore a narrative requirement, not decoration.
