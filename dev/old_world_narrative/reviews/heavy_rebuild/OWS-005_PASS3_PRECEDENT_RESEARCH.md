## FAO packing-house layout and operations

FAO's packing-house guidance defines a sequence of reception, cleaning/washing and special treatment, sorting/grading, packing, cooling/storage, assembly and dispatch. It recommends receiving at one end and dispatch at the other because incoming produce is dirty and crossing vehicle/product flows creates contamination and congestion.

Implications for OWS-005:

- the building needs a visually legible one-direction longitudinal process;
- raw receiving and finished dispatch must be separate thresholds;
- wash/sanitation is a real wet-service zone with drainage, not colored flooring;
- grading and packing require clear line space and staging rather than isolated props;
- culled/rejected product needs its own controlled exit.

References: [FAO packing houses and equipment](https://www.fao.org/4/T0073E/T0073E04.htm); [FAO packing-house operations](https://www.fao.org/4/x5014e/X5014e07.htm)

## USDA fresh-cut produce processor layout

USDA describes one-direction flow from raw to finished product, dedicated separation of raw, in-process and finished cold storage, hygiene controls at processing-room entrances, and air movement from the cleanest packaging areas toward less-clean receiving areas.

Implications:

- raw, wet, clean-pack and cold-hold masses should be architecturally distinct;
- staff hygiene/change thresholds and controlled doors belong at zone transitions;
- cold rooms should be enclosed, not represented by exposed cooler blocks;
- air-handling and sanitation services should follow the clean-to-dirty gradient;
- the clean-pack hall can use controlled glazing while cold storage remains window-poor.

Reference: [USDA Food Safety Practices to Expect from Your Fresh-Cut Produce Processor](https://www.fns.usda.gov/fs/produce-safety/processor)

## USDA regional food-hub facility program

USDA's food-hub guide identifies reception/loading, grading/repacking, delivery, dry packaging storage, subdivided cold/freezer rooms, offices, staff support, paved truck areas, ramps and covered docks as core facility components.

Implications:

- truck aprons and dock canopies are part of the architecture, not background decoration;
- packaging stock needs a dry clean route adjacent to packing;
- cold rooms may form a distinct insulated block with several temperature compartments;
- administration, staff support and vehicle circulation must fit alongside warehouse operations.

Reference: [USDA Regional Food Hub Resource Guide, Volume 2](https://www.rd.usda.gov/sites/default/files/usda-rd-foodhubs-vol2-final.pdf)

## FDA HACCP and sanitary-design questions

FDA HACCP guidance asks whether raw materials and ready-to-eat food are adequately separated, whether the facility and equipment can be consistently cleaned, and whether chilling, packaging integrity, case coding and storage conditions are controlled.

Implications:

- QA/sample/records space should overlook or adjoin the control points it documents;
- PT-9 sanitation and batch hold must arise from ordinary hazard-control architecture;
- floors, drains, service clearances and roof utilities should look cleanable and maintainable;
- the packaging quality report belongs near inspection/coding and controlled records.

Reference: [FDA HACCP Principles & Application Guidelines](https://www.fda.gov/food/hazard-analysis-critical-control-point-haccp/haccp-principles-application-guidelines)
