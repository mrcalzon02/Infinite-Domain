## Wageningen University & Research — high-throughput plant phenotyping

Wageningen describes high-throughput phenotyping as repeated, objective measurement of plant responses across climate chambers, greenhouses and field conditions. Its controlled facilities test drought, heat and salinity; automated greenhouse systems use conveyors and 3D scanning; measurements produce standardized time-series data used to select resilient varieties.

Architectural implications for OWS-007:

- reference and stress lots need comparable chamber modules rather than unrelated display cases;
- a visible transfer route should move plants/samples between controlled growth and scanning/phenotyping;
- drought, heat, salinity and recovery conditions can differ while sharing a service/measurement spine;
- observation and data review are physical parts of the experiment, not text-only lore;
- repeated chambers should communicate standardization, while distinct condition markers and service branches keep trials legible.

Reference: https://www.wur.nl/en/research/plant/high-throughput-phenotyping-faster-plant-breeding

## Wageningen Unifarm — controlled greenhouse and climate-room research

Unifarm combines high-tech greenhouses, climate-controlled rooms, quarantine-capable research, phenotyping, sample processing and data reporting in one supported plant-science facility. The relevant lesson is program diversity around controlled growing: chambers need preparation, specialized conditions, technical staff, measurement and downstream records.

Architectural implications:

- the facility requires more than grow cells;
- controlled greenhouses, climate rooms, spore-survival work and sample-processing rooms should form connected but bounded zones;
- maintenance and environmental systems must reach each chamber without using the public route;
- trial design, cultivation support, measurements and data reporting should have adjacent work nodes;
- specialized spore/persistence work may use stronger boundaries without making the entire pre-crisis institution look like an emergency bunker.

References:

- https://www.wur.nl/en/research/facilities/unifarm
- https://www.wur.nl/en/product/virtual-tour-serre-red.htm

## Kew Millennium Seed Bank — seed biology, ageing and germination workflow

Kew's Seed Biology Laboratories combine seed ripening, drying, cleaning, germination testing, comparative ageing, variable humidity, climate chambers, low-temperature storage, cryopreservation, imaging and chemical/biophysical analysis. The facility therefore demonstrates a chain from intake and conditioning through storage stress, viability testing and analysis rather than a single vault.

Architectural implications:

- accession, drying/conditioning, cleaning, storage, germination and analysis must be sequentially understandable;
- cold/dry storage needs visible environmental plant and controlled thresholds;
- accelerated ageing and humidity testing should sit beside, but not merge with, long-term retained stock;
- germination/reseeding is downstream verification, not decorative farmland outside the lab;
- imaging and analysis stations should compare treated samples against retained controls.

Reference: https://www.kew.org/science/collections-and-resources/research-facilities/laboratories/seed-biology-laboratories

## Kew Millennium Seed Bank — public observation without public laboratory access

Kew describes an exhibition zone between laboratory wings where visitors can observe seed research through glass while laboratories and the seed vault remain access-controlled.

Architectural implications:

- VCF's optimistic public showcase can be real architecture rather than signage;
- a protected visitor gallery can reveal the research chain while maintaining staff/sample separation;
- the public route should culminate in views of the most persuasive durability experiment, not cross active chambers;
- the contrast between polished exhibition and controlled back-of-house research supports the site's pre-crisis narrative.

Reference: https://www.kew.org/science/engage/accessing-our-science/access-our-collections/seed-collections

## USDA National Laboratory for Genetic Resources Preservation — secure storage and viability monitoring

USDA's preservation guidance uses moisture-proof packaging, freezer storage, cryogenic storage and monitor tests that trigger regeneration when viability declines. It also tracks accession origin and characteristics through a dedicated data system.

Architectural implications:

- retained control stock needs secure, environmentally controlled custody rather than generic crates;
- sample identity must follow material from accession through testing and release;
- storage and monitoring are linked: lots return from vaults to viability/reseeding tests;
- duplicate/retained lots and records should remain protected from routine visitor and waste routes;
- the proof record belongs where monitored results become a formal durability decision.

Reference: https://www.ars.usda.gov/ARSUserFiles/30123005/Images/Seed%20Brochure11182016.pdf

## Precedent synthesis for Minecraft

The governing relationships are:

- **accession -> conditioning -> retained control** before experimental exposure;
- **repeatable chamber modules + shared environmental services** for comparable trials;
- **controlled transfer -> imaging/phenotyping -> data review** rather than crops isolated from analysis;
- **storage ageing -> viability check -> reseeding** as a closed durability loop;
- **public observation separated from staff, sample, waste and maintenance circulation**;
- **secure retained stock and records** connected to release decisions;
- **clean, optimistic pre-crisis presentation** whose disturbing meaning comes from what the facility successfully measures.

These principles govern Pass 4 adjacency and later intact-state architecture. They do not assign a literal real-world biosafety or seed-bank certification to the fictional EP-7 program.
