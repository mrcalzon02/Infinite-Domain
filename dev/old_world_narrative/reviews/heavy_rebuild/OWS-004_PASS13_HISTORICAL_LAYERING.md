## Historical thesis

OWS-004 tells a different VCF story from the neighborhood depot, emergency grow hall and cold-chain nursery. This tower is proof that Evercrop-scale fungal agriculture had become **celebrated urban infrastructure**: a vertically integrated, publicly visible food-production building whose clean logistics, environmental systems, repeated cultivation floors and demonstration crown made industrial mycology look ordinary, efficient and desirable.

The later anomaly therefore must not turn the entire tower into an instant plague fortress. Its strongest historical beat is institutional confidence: one upper cultivation module is isolated while the rest of the facility continues to receive stock, grow product, harvest, pack and ship.

## D0 — Successful urban production

D0 is exactly the Gate-B r4 operating tower.

The building is in normal commercial service:

- the public/demo podium presents VCF cultivation as modern urban food security;
- clean culture/substrate receiving is separate from spent/dirty return;
- the primary staff stair, freight/service spine and west secondary egress all operate normally;
- four cultivation modules remain active;
- environmental and water/nutrient risers feed every production floor;
- harvest moves downward into grading/check, packing and dispatch;
- the rooftop greenhouse and environmental crown are productive/service spaces rather than decoration;
- the upper production module already possesses ordinary controlled boundaries and branchable environmental service, but those features read as professional process control rather than emergency quarantine.

D0 contains no crisis barricades, deterministic encounters, ruin damage or proof-placement distortions.

## D1 — Localized active containment while production continues

D1 represents a later and more explicit response than OWS-003's early gasket-quality anomaly. Staff have enough evidence to isolate the **upper cultivation module**, but the institution still treats the event as containable and continues operating the lower tower.

Required D1 transformations:

1. **Upper-floor vestibule becomes an active containment threshold.** Existing D0 doors/boundaries receive temporary seal layers, inspection markings and direct restricted-access language rather than a newly invented wall system.
2. **Upper environmental branch is isolated.** The already-present branch/shutoff anatomy gains a visible lockout/bypass state; lower-floor environmental service must remain intact and legible.
3. **Suspect production is segregated locally.** One or two grow-bank groups on the upper floor are taken out of ordinary flow; use contained stock, inspection staging and bounded cleanup material rather than generalized filth.
4. **Harvest routing changes.** The upper-floor harvest handoff is blocked/held while lower floors still feed the normal downward consolidation route.
5. **Staff circulation changes deliberately.** The principal staff stair still reaches all floors, but the upper-floor doorway becomes a controlled stop; freight/material transfer may be held at the upper level while remaining usable below.
6. **Emergency records/operating notices accumulate at the control point.** The evidence should communicate an institution attempting to isolate a production problem without shutting down the entire urban food asset.
7. **Public-facing optimism persists below.** The podium and demonstration areas are not retroactively converted into a disaster set; their normal appearance strengthens the contrast with the controlled upper-floor intervention.

D1 must preserve the lower three production floors as visibly operational. If the entire tower reads abandoned, burned, contaminated or militarized, D1 fails.

## D3 — Centuries-later ruin after abandonment

D3 is the modern worldgen ruin, long after the active-containment episode. The tower ultimately fails through abandonment and loss of building-scale environmental/water service, not because the upper anomaly explodes the structure.

Causal D3 priorities:

- **crown/environmental failure:** portions of greenhouse glazing and roof-service equipment fail first, exposing the top of the building to weather;
- **vertical-service degradation:** one major environmental/water riser segment is broken or missing, with corresponding floor-local service loss below it;
- **upper-module exposure:** the historically isolated upper cultivation level becomes the most visibly weathered production floor, combining old containment remnants with later water ingress and equipment loss;
- **selective facade/glazing failure:** damage follows production bays, roof edges, service penetrations and exposed corners rather than random air-block noise;
- **water path:** wet/mossed/cracked areas appear below crown/glazing failures and around service penetrations, with stronger effects higher in the tower and locally on lower floors where water would travel;
- **loading-edge weathering:** receiving, dirty-return and dispatch thresholds deteriorate more than protected internal/public zones;
- **cultivation-system attrition:** some rack/service sections survive, some are stripped/collapsed, and the four-floor production grammar remains reconstructable;
- **vertical circulation survival:** the principal stair and at least one secondary route remain readable enough for exploration; decay may interrupt convenience but may not make the deterministic proof path depend on destructive excavation;
- **podium persistence:** the public/demo face survives enough to show what the tower once advertised and celebrated.

## Historical continuity requirements

Across D1 and D3, preserve enough physical evidence to reconstruct:

`public normalization -> industrial vertical cultivation -> localized upper-floor containment -> continued lower-floor production -> later abandonment and building-service failure`.

The following must remain legible in D3:

- at least three of the four original cultivation levels as distinct production floors;
- the upper-floor containment boundary/remnants;
- the east freight/service anatomy;
- the principal staff stair as an institutional circulation system;
- the west secondary-egress identity;
- a recognizable rooftop greenhouse/environmental crown even if partly failed;
- public/demo podium versus receiving/dirty-return/dispatch separation;
- enough water/nutrient and environmental equipment to explain how the tower functioned;
- VCF ownership/institutional identity;
- the canonical proof route once Pass 17 is implemented.

## Forbidden historical shortcuts

Do not use:

- a single giant explosion hole;
- uniform moss/vines on every surface;
- every cultivation floor contaminated at D1;
- military barricades throughout the public podium;
- collapse that removes the tower's repeated production-floor identity;
- random facade subtraction with no relationship to roof, service or water failure;
- later Darknet mechanics as explanation for any heavy-rebuild feature.
