## 1. Orientation derived from the retained donor

For the Gate-A study, the existing orientation remains the working reference:

- **low Z / north:** primary administrative/front approach;
- **high Z / south:** rear service and outbound logistics side;
- **low X / west:** retained orchard and older agricultural site history;
- **high X / east:** principal technical/loading side of the converted plant;
- **central/east high volume:** retained cannery processing hall, to become the cold-chain hero volume.

The exact donor walls may move during massing, but the orientation is intentionally stable so the old agricultural property remains reconstructable beneath the VCF conversion.

## 2. Primary process spine

The building's normal physical sequence should read:

`east-side inbound receiving`

`-> intake condition / batch check`

`-> conditioned receiving hold`

`-> main cold vault / dormancy nursery assignment`

`-> release inspection`

`-> licensing / destination authorization`

`-> conditioned packing`

`-> outbound cold staging`

`-> south/rear dispatch`

This flow should move generally **from east/forward service intake through the main hall toward the rear dispatch side**, not loop freight through the front administrative wing.

## 3. Exception spine

The exception route should branch immediately from intake and remain close to both storage and service access:

`receiving / nursery flag`

`-> quality hold`

`-> seal / gasket inspection and repack`

`-> approved cold storage`

**or**

`-> isolated controlled outbound`

Quality hold must be close enough to receiving to intercept questionable material before it contaminates the normal workflow, but not inside the normal cold-vault aisle.

## 4. Staff and maintenance spine

A separate controlled staff/service route should connect:

`front/admin staff entry`

`-> batch/licensing records`

`-> operations corridor`

`-> release inspection`

`-> cold-vault/nursery service aisles`

`-> refrigeration/mechanical access`

`-> roof/service plant`

Technicians must be able to reach refrigeration plant and nursery service points without crossing the public/admin threshold or blocking freight movement.

## 6. Orchard/site zone

### Retained west orchard

The orchard should remain primarily a **historical/site-context zone**.

It may support:

- old agricultural service paths;
- remnants of former fruit-processing intake;
- limited staff/service edge access;
- visual evidence of the property's former function.

It should **not** become the main VCF culture-growing program. Doing so would blur the distinction between this cold-chain nursery and OWS-002's active cultivation role.

If Gate-A composition requires reducing or breaking the orchard canopy for better building legibility, preserve representative rows and site history rather than treating every donor tree as sacred.

## 7. Direct adjacency requirements

The following pairs should be directly adjacent or connected through a single controlled threshold:

- receiving <-> intake condition/batch check;
- intake check <-> conditioned receiving hold;
- intake check <-> quality hold;
- receiving hold <-> cold vault;
- cold vault <-> nursery cells;
- cold vault/nursery <-> release inspection;
- release inspection <-> licensing/batch records;
- release inspection <-> conditioned packing;
- conditioned packing <-> outbound cold staging;
- outbound cold staging <-> dispatch;

## 8. Near-adjacency requirements

These should be close but do not need a shared wall:

- supervisor/batch records near intake decision points;
- records near release inspection;
- quality hold near refrigeration service;
- replacement seal stock near quality hold/repack;
- staff support near admin but outside freight flow;
- maintenance storage near plant access.

## 9. Separation requirements

These should be intentionally separated:

- inbound receiving from outbound dispatch;
- suspect/quality-hold stock from released cold stock;
- front/admin movement from freight handling;
- exterior loading exposure from primary cold vault;
- orchard/context zone from clean biological storage;
- maintenance access from batch-release records;
- D1 temporary anomaly staging from permanent corporate identity/signage.

## 10. Buffer / transition spaces

Important buffers include:

- receiving vestibule / conditioned threshold;
- short receiving hold before main vault;
- controlled doors between vault/nursery and packing;
- inspection/release node before outbound staging;
- roof landing before plant access.

These transitions are part of the cold-chain architecture and should be visible.
