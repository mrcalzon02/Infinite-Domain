## Real-world precedent types studied

The rebuild used functional lessons from:

- FEMA short-term / evacuation shelter definitions, especially the conversion of existing schools, churches and community centers into emergency-support facilities;
- Logistics Cluster distribution-site guidance separating waiting, registration, handover, storage, staff functions, entry and exit;
- Logistics Cluster goods-flow guidance separating receiving, inspection, clean storage, exception/hold stock, picking, staging and dispatch;
- USDA controlled-environment agriculture references emphasizing water, nutrients, lighting/daylight, ventilation, vertical space and maintenance access;
- Penn State hydroponic nutrient-service practice covering stock solutions, circulation/pumping and actual utility support;
- emergency food-storage / warehouse guidance emphasizing receiving, protected storage, repacking, staging and outbound dispatch.

These references were used for workflow and adjacency rather than literal copying. The building intentionally retains a recognizable community-center shell beneath the VCF emergency conversion.

## Major architectural changes

The original structure was effectively a community-center donor with three crop strips and a few emergency props. The rebuild retains its useful civic identity while turning the building into a coherent emergency food-security institution.

The final architecture includes:

- retained civic public entrance and community-building proportions;
- a lower west support wing and north public/admin bar;
- a taller clear-span grow hall as the dominant hero volume;
- retained roof lantern used as cultivation/daylight architecture;
- explicit structural bay rhythm and high clerestories aligned with the hall interior;
- a composed east receiving/batch-check threshold;
- a composed south relief-dispatch threshold and external handoff lane;
- physically legible rooftop grow-service plant;
- a visible service connection from rooftop equipment into the hall irrigation network;
- differentiated public and back-of-house elevations rather than one generic civic box.

Gate A r1 was rejected because the lower civic mass competed with the grow hall and the service additions read as thin attachments. Gate A r2 corrected the hierarchy before interior work proceeded.

`public approach -> registration / authorization -> culture / relief issue`

`receiving -> batch check -> clean culture stock -> controlled cultivation -> harvest transfer -> wash/check -> packing/staging -> relief dispatch`.

The facility contains:

- public queue and registration/authorization threshold;

## Historical / collapse story

### D0 — successful emergency operation

The civic facility has been professionally converted to relieve food insecurity. Cultivation, receiving, harvest processing, packing and public distribution all work simultaneously.

### D1 — demand pressure and bounded quality anomaly

D1 changes exactly **83 structure positions** from D0.

The site remains operational while:

- public-side relief backlog grows;
- receiving and dispatch carry more stock;
- the south/east end of Rack 3 is isolated under a quality hold;
- replacement/inspection material accumulates on the service side.

The anomaly is deliberately local. The site does not become an instant quarantine fortress.

### D3 — abandonment and centuries-later causal ruin

Gate-C r1 initially changed 352 positions but failed visual review because the exterior remained too pristine.

Gate-C r2 preserves D0 and D1 exactly and increases D3 to **520 changed positions** through causal deterioration:

- roof-lantern glazing failure while the frame survives;
- west/lower civic-roof membrane failures spreading from the wet/service side;
- visible west wet-service exterior deterioration beneath those failures;
- historically suspect Rack 3 failing more heavily than the other cultivation banks;
- interrupted local irrigation branch while the larger system remains reconstructable;
- selective hall masonry and clerestory losses placed between surviving structural bay lines;
- more visible east receiving apron/canopy weathering;
- more visible south dispatch outer-canopy/apron weathering;
- sparse debris beneath actual failure areas;
- comparatively protected public/records frontage.

The final ruin therefore follows loss of maintenance, water entry, glazing failure and long weather exposure rather than implying that one suspect crop rack blew up the building.

## Environmental storytelling

Before reading any proof item, the player can infer that:

- an ordinary civic building was converted because food security had become an emergency problem;
- VCF cultivation technology was deployed as practical public infrastructure;
- cultures and nutrient stock arrived through a controlled service route;

- later physical ruin primarily resulted from abandonment and weathering.

The unique proof item confirms and deepens this environmental evidence rather than substituting for it.
