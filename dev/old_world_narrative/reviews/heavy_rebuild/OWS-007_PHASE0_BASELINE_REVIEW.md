## Overall reading

The baseline reads as a large rectangular technical annex attached to a dominant cylindrical reactor/containment building. It does not read first as agricultural biotechnology.

The box-and-cylinder composition is more distinctive than a generic laboratory cube, so total demolition is not automatically warranted. However, the surviving silhouette and interior organization communicate the nuclear donor far more strongly than the intended EP-7 durability-development program.

## Useful elements visible in the baseline

- The large laboratory hall, lower annex and cylinder create three legible masses that could support differentiated public/research/service functions.
- The cylinder is a memorable hero-space opportunity if its nuclear identity is fully converted into an agricultural stress-test/environmental simulation chamber.
- The north entrance canopy is visible and gives later planning a principal public/security threshold.
- The repeated roof-light strips could plausibly serve controlled cultivation, observation or daylight-assisted work.
- The cutaway confirms that framed-glass test cells, cooler/storage blocks and a bridge/core relationship exist rather than being text-only claims.
- The site apron leaves room to express separate visitor, sample-receiving, cold-chain/service and waste movements.

## Blocking exterior findings for later planning

1. **Wrong first reading.** The cylindrical volume, reinforced-glass dome and window-poor rectangular halls read as a reactor annex or power-research compound, not crop development.
2. **VCF identity is applied rather than architectural.** Thin white/lime bands do not alter the donor's massing, thresholds, roof plant or facade logic.
3. **No agricultural research silhouette.** There is no legible greenhouse/test-bay hierarchy, environmental-service spine, shade/screen system, visible cultivation wing or sample logistics anatomy.
4. **The cylinder is unresolved.** It is visually dominant but still expresses a reactor vessel. Unless later planning gives it a precise EP-7 testing function, it will remain narrative contradiction rather than a retained donor asset.
5. **Broad blank elevations dominate.** Large dark/light-gray wall fields have little structural bay, research glazing, controlled openings, service access or human-scale articulation.
6. **Threshold hierarchy is weak.** Public entry, secure staff access, sample receiving, cold-store handling, dispatch and waste/service egress are not distinguishable from the exterior.
7. **Roof composition is donor-led.** The roof lights are useful raw rhythm, but there is no coherent environmental-control plant, nutrient/water service, maintenance path or connection to the trial cells below.
8. **The site is operationally empty.** The apron and landscape do not explain how research samples, staff, cold-chain materials and waste reach or leave the facility.

## Blocking interior / sectional findings for later planning

The cutaway shows four glass grow cells and several large equipment islands, but it does not demonstrate a full agricultural-development workflow.

Missing or illegible relationships include:

- controlled seed/sample accession and batch registration;
- clean sample preparation before testing;
- a baseline/control cultivation zone distinct from stress trials;
- environmental test categories with separate utility requirements;
- observation routes that do not cross test-cell service routes;
- spore-survival testing and contamination-control boundaries;
- storage-duration and cold-chain trial sequence;
- reseeding evaluation and comparative result review;
- food-quality review before commercial release;
- staff support, sanitation, maintenance and waste routes;
- credible vertical and roof-service access for a 33-block-high facility;
- a secure records/decision node appropriate to the guaranteed proof.

The four current crop cells are credible program seeds, but they occupy too little of the building and are not connected to a visible technical system. The cooler and crate banks likewise read as placed props rather than a coordinated storage-trial workflow.

## Historical and narrative implications

OWS-007 is explicitly pre-crisis. The absence of generalized ruin is correct and should be preserved in the intact interpretation. Its discomfort should emerge from ordinary corporate success:

- trial categories optimized for survival through drought, cold, transport and long storage;
- spore persistence and reseeding success treated as release criteria;
- polished observation and product-review spaces that celebrate those traits;
- deterministic records proving that persistence was designed and approved.

Later history must not turn the site into an OWS-008 containment-incident duplicate. The architecture should first show the normal institution clearly enough that eventual age/abandonment reads as transformation of a successful research campus.
