## Overall reading

The baseline reads as a substantial, window-poor mountain biohazard laboratory with a pitched public intake, stepped wings, roof monitors and three shielded exhaust stacks. Its inherited laboratory identity is stronger than a generic box and gives the rebuild credible containment bones.

It does not yet read specifically as a VCF emergency persistence-investigation facility. Most of the exterior remains the brown/dark-gray donor, while the target identity is limited to narrow facade bands and colored lower openings. The crisis-investigation story is much more visible in the cutaway than in the building/site composition.

## Useful elements visible in the baseline

- The stepped public, research, containment and rear-support volumes create a legible institutional silhouette worth studying rather than discarding automatically.
- The pitched north intake and canopy establish a principal approach distinct from the window-poor technical wings.
- Multiple roof levels, monitors and exhaust stacks offer a credible basis for pressure-zone and sterilization plant expression.
- The cutaway confirms four repeated glass bays, colored thresholds, service strips and a deeper research/records plan; the program is not only a text claim.
- The floor slices show existing partitions, two-level research occupancy and a substantial containment/service wing that can support real clean/dirty separation.
- The site has an east loading/service edge and south emergency/utility opportunity distinct from the public approach.

## Exterior findings

1. **Institutional identity is applied, not architectural.** Lime/white accents do not substantially change the mountain biohazard donor's silhouette, facade hierarchy, thresholds or plant anatomy.
2. **Emergency investigation is not the first reading.** The exterior communicates a hardened generic laboratory, but not repeated sterilization, clean-zone verification, service-joint inspection or VCF crisis conversion.
3. **Public and operational thresholds remain under-expressed.** The main intake is legible, but contaminated specimen receipt, staff clean entry, sterilant/service delivery, waste removal and emergency egress are not distinguishable as a complete operating system.
4. **Roof systems are generic.** Monitors and stacks provide useful massing, yet intake/exhaust segregation, decontamination plant, sterilant/wash utilities and maintenance circulation are not connected visibly to the test zones below.
5. **Site infrastructure is sparse.** Retaining shoulders and paths seat the building, but there is no convincing incident-response staging, controlled receiving, waste hold or utility/service yard.
6. **Facade rhythm is donor-led.** Broad blank walls and small colored openings dominate; containment-zone numbering, progressive retrofit layers and VCF bioscience character are not embedded into architecture.

## Interior / sectional findings

The cutaway gives the overlay its strongest reading: four glass enclosures form a repeated experimental sequence. However, the sequence is spatially too uniform and its operational boundaries are incomplete.

Missing or illegible relationships include:

- contaminated specimen receipt and chain of custody;
- dirty-side examination before sterilization;
- personnel change, shower/wash and material pass-throughs between pressure zones;
- distinct sterilization methods/cycles rather than four visually identical rooms;
- post-treatment clean hold and verification separated from active challenge testing;
- observation/control stations that do not share the specimen route;
- service-wall and joint-access corridors where hidden recurrence can be discovered;
- segregated air, wash, sterilant and drainage feeds for each test zone;
- retained-sample storage and comparative incident chronology;
- investigation command and a secure archive/cache appropriate to the proof and later Darknet return;
- waste return and removal without recrossing clean circulation.

The current glass bays are credible functional seeds, but they replace a large portion of the donor plan with repeated shells. Thin pipe runs, small mycelium seams and crate/equipment rows read as props rather than a coordinated persistence-investigation system.

## Historical and narrative implications

OWS-008 is already in active containment. Its intact interpretation must therefore differ from both a normal commercial laboratory and a final ruin. The architecture should retain VCF's earlier bioscience professionalism while showing successive emergency layers:

- original VCF rooms repurposed for incident investigation;
- numbered clean/dirty boundaries and separately logged sterilization zones;
- successive seals or access panels at service joints;
- inspection routes opened behind walls and under floors;
- a controlled place to compare negative surface tests against positive joint samples;
- an archive that records repeated failures without making lore text carry the entire story.

The present mushrooms along repeated seams state the intended fact, but they do not yet demonstrate how staff found, tested and unsuccessfully corrected the recurrence.
