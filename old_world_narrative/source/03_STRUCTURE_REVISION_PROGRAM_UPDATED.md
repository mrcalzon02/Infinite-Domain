# 03 — Structure Revision Program

## Target count

Implement **64** narrative structure variants. This is deliberately inside the requested 50–75 range.

The matrix provides narrative targets, not assumptions about the current schematic filenames. Codex must map those targets to the final reviewed structure catalog.

## Acceptance test for a narrative revision

A structure variant must materially change at least **four of six** dimensions below, and quest-critical/rare structures should usually change five or six:

1. **Silhouette / exterior identity** — recognizable industrial, medical, logistics, military, scientific, civic, or aerospace function.
2. **Interior zoning / circulation** — rooms and paths reflect the facility's real operational purpose and its emergency history.
3. **Functional machinery / props** — tanks, belts, storage, test arrays, clinic equipment, server rooms, power equipment, decontamination, etc.
4. **Institutional identity** — recurring logos, labels, safety language, packaging, uniforms/containers, department naming.
5. **Historical damage signature** — damage reflects *when and how* this facility failed rather than generic random rubble.
6. **Narrative evidence / loot** — deterministic proof item plus appropriate records, signs, spatial clues, and practical loot.

## Collapse-phase visual overlays

Use these as a reusable damage grammar:

### Phase A — Pre-crisis / normal operation
- clean or maintained;
- coherent workflow;
- optimistic corporate/public language;
- no unnecessary barricades;
- ordinary maintenance clutter rather than apocalypse clutter.

### Phase B — Early anomaly
- maintenance escalation;
- isolated failed seals/filters;
- temporary warning signs;
- one or two improvised substitutions;
- partial shutdowns while most of the facility remains normal.

### Phase C — Active containment
- screening areas;
- decontamination;
- temporary walls;
- rerouted corridors;
- polymer restrictions;
- protective equipment piles;
- emergency staffing and material substitution.

### Phase D — Late containment / Firebreak
- military authority;
- sealed traffic lanes;
- heavy barriers;
- burn damage;
- evacuation evidence;
- blast preparation or observation systems;
- documentation that treats drastic measures as necessary operations.

### Phase E — Post-collapse
- long-term structural decay;
- Spore occupation appropriate to biome/location;
- scavenging;
- weather intrusion;
- ecological reclamation;
- no implication that all damage happened in one dramatic moment.

## Institution design languages

Do not force every building into a single post-apocalyptic palette. Establish reusable modular languages.

### Verdant Continuum Foods (VCF)
Operational logic: cultivation, nutrient flow, harvesting, storage, packaging, cold chain, agricultural biotech.

Recurring elements: cultivation levels, chamber numbers, nutrient return lines, hygienic but industrial surfaces, farm control rooms, VCF packaging, culture storage, early clean facilities that later show unsettling internal infiltration.

Failure signature: contamination in service joints, drainage, seals, filters, and supposedly separated cultivation systems.

### Atlas Kinetic Industries
Operational logic: automation, material handling, Create-like mechanical production, industrial maintenance.

Recurring elements: conveyors/belts, presses, crushers, gear-driven systems, machine access lanes, maintenance cages, parts storage, lift systems.

Failure signature: facilities mechanically intact but starved of maintenance, power, seals, or safe operators; later sites show emergency cannibalization.

### PolyCore Advanced Materials
Operational logic: controlled material testing and repeated substitution.

Recurring elements: test arrays, exposure chambers, material labels, sample racks, failure-time boards, ceramic/metal isolation retrofits.

Failure signature: progression from ordinary polymers to increasingly exotic barriers. Let room sequences physically demonstrate the scientific escalation.

### Pleroma Distribution Group
Operational logic: flow, sorting, cold chain, standardized cargo, massive repetition.

Recurring elements: loading bays, container identifiers, freight lanes, racking, automated sorting, refrigerated areas, customs/inspection spaces.

Failure signature: blocked flows, abandoned sealed food, quarantine overlays, containers reused as barriers, evidence that distribution itself became a vector.

### Aevum Therapeutics
Operational logic: premium clinical care and biomedical research.

Recurring elements: calm treatment rooms, monitoring, clean recovery wards, high-end branding, laboratories, controlled storage, later emergency overflow.

Failure signature: dependency—patients and systems that cannot simply be shut down when supply fails. The facilities should communicate genuine benefit before tragedy.

### Helion Energy Systems
Operational logic: grid stability, power conversion, coolant, high-voltage distribution, nuclear infrastructure.

Recurring elements: fenced utility compounds, switchgear, pumps, coolant/service corridors, control rooms, heavy industrial safety zones.

Failure signature: small seal/insulation/coolant problems cascading into larger outages and service improvisation.

### Blackglass Information Systems
Operational logic: secure computation, networking, archives, restricted access, data continuity.

Recurring elements: access-controlled rooms, server/data halls, hardened utilities, encrypted terminals, physical data modules, network exchange rooms.

Failure signature: physically valuable sites whose narrative value is partially locked until Darknet capability. Preserve reasons to revisit.

### Asterion Orbital Industries
Operational logic: clean aerospace production, mission control, launch logistics, orbital communications.

Recurring elements: controlled assembly bays, launch support, tracking/communications, specialized cargo, clean rooms, mission markings.

Failure signature: evacuation, interrupted launch operations, off-world continuity, records that survive because they left Earth's normal information systems.

### Continuity
Continuity is not a monolithic corporation. It should visually look like **multiple institutions forced together**.

Recurring elements: VCF reports beside PolyCore test data, atmospheric maps, military orders, improvised cross-reference boards, reused offices, temporary secure archives, mixed equipment, handwritten annotations.

Failure signature: increasing urgency and evidence synthesis, not a secret-superlab aesthetic everywhere.

## Environmental evidence first

At least one major story point per quest-grade structure should be understandable without opening a book. Examples include:

- fungus on both sides of a quarantine wall;
- replacement seals stacked beside the same failed pump;
- progressive material test chambers from polymer to ceramic to metal;
- sealed Evercrop cargo abandoned in a logistics train/warehouse;
- a clean longevity poster beside rationing notices;
- fire damage followed by later fungal occupation;
- a breached 'sterile' service conduit rather than a smashed front door;
- successful Firebreak signage near evidence of distant contamination.

## Rarity and density

Follow the canon's escalation:

- **Common:** small freight, utility, shelters, minor cultivation, small checkpoints.
- **Uncommon:** vertical farms, hospitals, labs, outposts, ports.
- **Rare:** major research, large containment, data centers, Firebreak command sites.
- **Landmark:** Continuity Archive, major Firebreak crater/command complex, major Asterion launch complex, equivalent late-game anchors.

Do not make all 64 structures huge or individually rare. Narrative density comes from many small repeated clues plus a few landmarks.

## Alternative structures — regional faction architecture

The central-continent structure program remains authoritative. The following is an **additional regional structure division**, not a replacement for the 64 narrative variants above.

These regional families should reuse the same acceptance standards, collapse-phase overlays, environmental storytelling rules, rarity logic, loot logic, and quest-integration expectations already defined in this document. Their purpose is to make travel beyond the central continent immediately legible through architecture, infrastructure, settlement planning, industrial organization, and repeated material language.

Do not treat these as simple palette swaps. A structure assigned to one of these regional families should be re-authored far enough that the player can identify the originating polity from silhouette, planning logic, building materials, signage treatment, utilities, defensive assumptions, and interior organization before reading explicit faction text.

### World placement

Use the central continent as the primary reference region.

- **The Karsic Directorate:** preferentially spawn on the **right/eastern side of the world map**, beyond the central continent and its immediate transition zone.
- **The Pelagos Compact:** preferentially spawn on the **left/western side of the world map**, beyond the central continent and its immediate transition zone.
- The separation should be strong enough to create recognizable regional identity, but not so absolute that every border becomes mechanically perfect.
- A small number of displaced, abandoned, diplomatic, expeditionary, military, logistics, or trade structures may appear outside their home region when the placement makes narrative sense.
- Cross-regional structures should be deliberately rare and should communicate *why* they are present rather than weakening regional readability through random mixing.
- Transition areas may contain contested sites, imported equipment, mixed cargo, captured structures, foreign observation posts, or infrastructure whose ownership changed during the collapse.

The result should make geographic travel feel like movement between different historical and political development zones rather than movement through one endlessly repeated architectural civilization.

## The Karsic Directorate

### Regional identity

The Karsic Directorate should have a **distinctly Russian-leaning structural language**, drawing from Soviet and post-Soviet civic, industrial, scientific, military, rail, utility, and settlement architecture without becoming a parody or a collection of superficial national symbols.

The strongest inspiration should come from the built environment: monumental civic scale, heavy public works, standardized residential construction, large industrial footprints, rail-oriented logistics, severe-weather engineering, district heating, concrete utility infrastructure, hardened scientific facilities, practical military construction, and repeated state-standard design families.

The Directorate should feel like a society accustomed to building systems at continental scale and expecting infrastructure to survive cold, distance, logistical isolation, and long periods of imperfect maintenance.

### Exterior design language

Recurring exterior elements should include:

- large concrete, brick, stone, and steel masses;
- broad rectangular silhouettes with strong horizontal or vertical repetition;
- prefabricated concrete panel construction for common residential and administrative buildings;
- enclosed service corridors and weather-protected connections where climate warrants them;
- heavy exterior piping, district-heating lines, utility gantries, pump stations, substations, and centralized service infrastructure;
- deep-set windows, practical glazing, storm protection, and visibly repairable exterior systems;
- substantial foundations and raised mechanical elements in areas affected by snow, frozen soil, flooding, or industrial contamination;
- oversized rail yards, loading aprons, gantry cranes, storage depots, maintenance sheds, and freight transfer facilities;
- monumental government, research, military, and transportation buildings whose scale exceeds what the surviving population could now reasonably maintain;
- standardized fences, lamps, barriers, stair systems, service doors, shelters, and utility sheds that recur across otherwise unrelated sites.

Avoid making every Directorate site uniformly brutalist. Older masonry districts, industrial brickwork, utilitarian steel structures, prefabricated housing, later glass-and-metal retrofits, rural technical buildings, underground facilities, and monumental state construction should coexist.

### Settlement and planning logic

Karsic settlements should often read as planned districts rather than individually accumulated buildings.

Useful patterns include:

- apartment blocks arranged around courtyards, service roads, schools, clinics, heating plants, and communal facilities;
- industrial settlements built around one dominant mine, rail yard, reactor, factory, refinery, launch site, hydroelectric installation, or research complex;
- long axial roads or ceremonial approaches terminating at administrative or institutional buildings;
- centralized utility buildings serving multiple surrounding structures;
- rail spurs penetrating deep into industrial and military compounds;
- large open staging areas intended for trucks, maintenance equipment, containers, military vehicles, or seasonal stockpiles;
- underground or hardened service spaces that become disproportionately important after the collapse.

The player should repeatedly encounter evidence that a Directorate settlement was designed as part of a system rather than as a collection of independent properties.

### Interior language

Interiors should favor durable, standardized, maintainable spaces:

- long corridors and repeated room modules;
- tiled or painted concrete service areas;
- heavy doors and vestibules;
- communal changing rooms and wash areas in industrial facilities;
- centralized control rooms overlooking large mechanical spaces;
- repair workshops with extensive spare-parts storage;
- basement utilities, pipe galleries, electrical rooms, shelters, archive rooms, and secondary service passages;
- institutional signage organized by building section, floor, technical function, or numbered department;
- furniture and equipment that appear standardized across multiple Directorate institutions.

Later or prestige facilities may show modernization layered over older infrastructure rather than complete replacement: new computers inside older control rooms, modern laboratories connected to legacy utility tunnels, upgraded doors installed in old concrete frames, and high-technology equipment supported by visibly older mechanical systems.

### Directorate institutional variants

Existing narrative institutions may appear in Directorate territory, but their local facilities should be regionally adapted.

Examples:

- **VCF-linked cultivation:** massive heated greenhouse blocks, insulated vertical farms, agricultural research stations, seed-storage bunkers, nutrient plants, rail-fed food depots.
- **Atlas-linked industry:** heavy machine halls, foundries, repair combines, centralized tool depots, rail-served manufacturing districts, large mechanical transfer systems.
- **PolyCore-linked research:** concrete materials institutes, isolated testing halls, underground sample storage, pressure chambers, environmental test facilities.
- **Pleroma-linked logistics:** rail classification yards, inland freight terminals, cold stores, bonded warehouses, standardized container districts.
- **Aevum-linked medicine:** district hospitals, sanatorium-like recovery facilities, medical research campuses, emergency wards retrofitted into large civic buildings.
- **Helion-linked energy:** district heating plants, hydroelectric control works, reactor service facilities, substations, turbine halls, high-capacity transmission compounds.
- **Blackglass-linked information:** hardened communications exchanges, underground data rooms, state archive facilities, remote relay stations, secure technical institutes.
- **Asterion-linked aerospace:** remote tracking stations, launch-support rail yards, hardened telemetry facilities, assembly halls, isolated cosmodrome-style logistics zones.

### Directorate collapse signature

Karsic collapse storytelling should emphasize **systems continuing far beyond the point at which the society operating them was healthy enough to sustain them**.

Recurring evidence may include:

- centralized heating or water systems keeping several buildings alive while adjacent districts freeze or flood;
- heroic maintenance logs showing crews repeatedly repairing the same pumps, seals, substations, boilers, or rail systems;
- cannibalized machinery where one facility was sacrificed to keep another operational;
- evacuation plans built around trains, buses, barges, or centralized assembly points that became impossible to execute;
- sealed technical basements that remained functional after public areas failed;
- food, fuel, and medical stockpiles concentrated in large depots that became strategic choke points;
- improvised military control of transport corridors and industrial compounds;
- later settlements occupying only small inhabitable portions of enormous pre-collapse facilities.

The emotional tone should be less "everything instantly shattered" and more "the machine kept running while its margins disappeared."

### Directorate structure families

Codex should develop Directorate-specific revisions or alternates across enough categories that the region cannot be represented by two or three landmark structures alone.

Priority families include:

- prefabricated apartment blocks and courtyard housing;
- workers' dormitories;
- municipal administrative buildings;
- schools and technical institutes;
- district clinics and hospitals;
- heating plants and boiler houses;
- water pumping and treatment stations;
- electrical substations;
- rail stations and freight depots;
- classification yards and maintenance sheds;
- industrial machine halls;
- mines and ore-handling facilities;
- refineries and chemical works;
- food storage and agricultural combines;
- military checkpoints and hardened outposts;
- communications stations;
- scientific research institutes;
- underground shelters and technical basements;
- river, reservoir, and hydroelectric infrastructure;
- remote aerospace tracking or launch-support sites.

Common structures should carry the identity just as strongly as rare landmarks.

## The Pelagos Compact

### Regional identity

The Pelagos Compact should occupy the left/western region and use a **modern British-leaning structural language**.

The goal is not faux-medieval Britain, fantasy cottages, or an endless field of Victorian buildings. The strongest reference should be the layering visible in modern British towns and infrastructure: older masonry and brick structures repeatedly adapted for new uses, dense mixed-use streets, terraced and semi-detached housing, post-war civic construction, modern glass-and-steel additions, rail infrastructure, maritime facilities, compact industrial estates, utility buildings, institutional campuses, and a strong habit of fitting new systems into constrained inherited spaces.

Pelagos should feel more spatially compressed and incrementally modified than the Directorate. Where Karsic infrastructure often announces a large coordinated system, Pelagos infrastructure should frequently show generations of additions, extensions, conversions, bypasses, service alleys, annexes, and retrofits.

### Exterior design language

Recurring exterior elements should include:

- brick as a major visual anchor, mixed with stone, concrete, steel, render, and later glass additions;
- terraced, semi-detached, row, and compact block housing;
- narrow service alleys and rear utility access;
- pitched roofs mixed with flat-roofed post-war and contemporary additions;
- converted older buildings whose present function is visibly newer than the shell;
- rail arches, retaining walls, embankments, platforms, signal structures, maintenance compounds, and compact stations;
- dense high streets and mixed civic/commercial frontage where appropriate;
- compact industrial estates with warehouses, workshops, fenced yards, loading areas, and office fronts;
- maritime quays, ferry infrastructure, seawalls, warehouses, breakwaters, lifeboat facilities, port offices, and coastal utilities;
- modern institutional buildings that preserve fragments of older campuses around them;
- restrained signage, wayfinding, municipal street furniture, bollards, fencing, lamp standards, shelters, and standardized public-service elements.

Modern structures should coexist with older building stock rather than erasing it. A twenty-first-century laboratory may occupy a campus containing a converted brick warehouse, a 1960s concrete wing, and a newer glass connector.

### Settlement and planning logic

Pelagos settlements should often reflect constrained geography, inherited property lines, older roads, rail corridors, coasts, rivers, and repeated redevelopment.

Useful patterns include:

- dense town centers with irregular older street grids;
- rows of attached housing leading into later suburban or post-war estates;
- civic buildings inserted into existing urban blocks;
- rail lines dividing neighborhoods and creating bridges, tunnels, arches, service roads, and awkward industrial pockets;
- compact logistics facilities with limited maneuvering space compared with Directorate equivalents;
- ports tightly integrated with towns rather than isolated as enormous standalone freight districts;
- industrial parks developed in phases, producing visibly different building generations;
- coastal defenses and flood infrastructure embedded into promenades, docks, roads, and utilities;
- repeated building conversions caused by scarcity of land and pressure to reuse existing structures.

Pelagos should communicate that the society rarely had the luxury of clearing everything and rebuilding from zero.

### Interior language

Interiors should frequently show adaptation:

- old masonry shells containing modern partitions and services;
- narrow corridors created by repeated subdivision;
- plant rooms squeezed into extensions, rooftops, basements, or rear yards;
- cable trays and piping routed through buildings that were never designed for them;
- multiple generations of doors, windows, fire systems, lifts, access controls, and signage;
- converted warehouses serving as laboratories, clinics, offices, emergency shelters, or data facilities;
- compact control rooms and maintenance areas;
- institutional spaces where older ceremonial or civic rooms sit beside highly practical modern additions;
- storage in every leftover niche once supply chains begin failing.

Prestige facilities may be sleek in selected areas, but their back-of-house spaces should often reveal older infrastructure underneath.

### Compact institutional variants

Existing narrative institutions may appear in Compact territory, but their facilities should reflect Pelagos planning and reuse.

Examples:

- **VCF-linked cultivation:** rooftop growing systems, converted industrial greenhouses, compact vertical farms, glasshouse research campuses, peri-urban agricultural stations.
- **Atlas-linked industry:** compact workshops, industrial estates, rail-arch fabrication sites, converted mills, port maintenance works, modular production halls.
- **PolyCore-linked research:** university-adjacent laboratories, converted technical colleges, older research buildings with modern containment wings.
- **Pleroma-linked logistics:** port warehouses, ferry freight terminals, compact distribution parks, rail-connected depots, refrigerated urban logistics centers.
- **Aevum-linked medicine:** dense urban hospitals, repurposed civic buildings, clinic networks, modern treatment wings attached to older medical campuses.
- **Helion-linked energy:** coastal power facilities, substations embedded in towns, compact utility compounds, offshore-energy support yards, converted industrial plants.
- **Blackglass-linked information:** telecom exchanges, repurposed office blocks, secure basement data facilities, coastal communications stations, university-linked computing centers.
- **Asterion-linked aerospace:** aerospace engineering parks, satellite-control facilities, coastal tracking installations, repurposed airfields, compact assembly and research campuses.

### Compact collapse signature

Pelagos collapse storytelling should emphasize **interdependence, constrained space, and successive retrofits overwhelming systems that were already layered and tightly coupled**.

Recurring evidence may include:

- quarantine routes forced through old street networks that were impossible to cleanly isolate;
- hospitals expanding into schools, offices, halls, stations, or adjacent civic buildings;
- warehouses converted repeatedly from commercial storage to ration depot to medical supply center to evacuation shelter;
- flood barriers, checkpoints, and emergency utilities occupying already narrow streets;
- rail and ferry systems becoming essential evacuation arteries and therefore dangerous concentration points;
- modern containment equipment attached to structures with much older drains, ducts, basements, or service passages;
- contamination bypassing new barriers through forgotten legacy infrastructure;
- neighborhood-level improvisation because centralized facilities cannot physically absorb all demand;
- old brick or stone structures surviving structurally while their modern services fail around them.

The Compact should often look as though each solution was forced to coexist with three previous solutions.

### Compact structure families

Codex should develop Pelagos-specific revisions or alternates across a broad everyday structure pool.

Priority families include:

- terraced and compact urban housing;
- semi-detached and post-war residential estates;
- mixed-use high-street buildings;
- council or municipal offices;
- schools, colleges, and university-linked facilities;
- dense hospitals and clinics;
- rail stations, depots, signal buildings, and maintenance yards;
- ferry terminals and small passenger ports;
- commercial docks and warehouses;
- seawalls, coastal pumping, flood-control, and utility structures;
- compact industrial estates;
- converted mills and warehouses;
- logistics parks and refrigerated storage;
- telecom exchanges and data facilities;
- police, emergency, and civil-defense sites;
- laboratories integrated into older campuses;
- airfields and aerospace engineering sites;
- coastal observation and communications stations.

Again, the identity must appear frequently in ordinary structures rather than only in rare landmarks.

## Regional structure equivalency and variation

The Directorate and Compact should not require entirely separate gameplay taxonomies for every structure. Instead, establish **regional equivalencies**.

A central-continent "small hospital," for example, may have:

- a central-continent corporate or municipal variant;
- a Karsic district-clinic or institutional hospital variant;
- a Pelagos dense urban hospital or converted civic-medical variant.

A freight structure may become:

- a central Pleroma warehouse;
- a Karsic rail-oriented freight combine;
- a Pelagos compact port or rail distribution depot.

A data site may become:

- a central Blackglass data center;
- a Karsic hardened communications/archive facility;
- a Pelagos telecom exchange or converted secure computing block.

The gameplay role may remain comparable while silhouette, circulation, infrastructure, environmental storytelling, and regional loot presentation change substantially.

Where practical, use these equivalencies to increase world variety without multiplying unique mechanics beyond what the pack can maintain.

## Regional rarity and density

Regional identity must be visible primarily through **common and uncommon structures**, not reserved for rare landmarks.

Suggested distribution logic:

- **Common regional identity structures:** homes, utility buildings, small shops, workshops, shelters, minor rail or road infrastructure, local service buildings.
- **Uncommon regional identity structures:** hospitals, schools, farms, medium industrial sites, freight depots, rail stations, ports, municipal facilities, laboratories.
- **Rare regional identity structures:** major research institutes, large military facilities, hardened data centers, major industrial complexes, regional command facilities.
- **Regional landmarks:** exceptionally large Directorate infrastructure anchors or Compact civic/port/research anchors that can serve as exploration-chain destinations.

The existing global rarity rules remain in force. Regional variants should inherit the rarity of the underlying gameplay function unless there is a specific narrative reason to change it.

## Exploration and quest integration

Do not replace the existing exploration quest chain. Add regional discovery objectives to it.

Regional exploration should gradually teach the player that architecture itself is a navigation and lore system.

Examples of suitable additions include:

- identify and enter the first clearly Directorate-built structure;
- locate a Directorate rail or district-utility complex;
- locate a Pelagos rail, port, or converted industrial structure;
- recover records demonstrating a different regional response to the same collapse phase;
- compare two versions of a shared institution operating under different regional conditions;
- locate a rare foreign structure displaced into the wrong region and determine why it was there;
- reach a major regional landmark after the player has learned enough visual language to recognize its importance before entering it.

Quest text should point the player toward structures that carry meaningful lore, but the structures must remain understandable through environmental evidence even when the player ignores or postpones the written material.

## Codex implementation rule

When the reviewed schematic catalog is available, Codex should classify candidate structures into:

1. central-continent baseline;
2. Karsic Directorate candidate;
3. Pelagos Compact candidate;
4. cross-regional / expeditionary candidate;
5. unsuitable without major rebuild.

Do not simply recolor or relabel a baseline schematic and count it as a regional variant.

A Directorate or Compact conversion should still satisfy the document's normal revision acceptance test and should materially change the building's regional identity in addition to its narrative function.

If a schematic is structurally useful but aesthetically generic, prefer rebuilding its exterior language, utility layout, signage, access patterns, and interior circulation around the selected regional family while retaining only the portions worth salvaging.

The finished world should allow an experienced player to infer approximately where they are in the broader map from the structures around them even before opening the map itself.
