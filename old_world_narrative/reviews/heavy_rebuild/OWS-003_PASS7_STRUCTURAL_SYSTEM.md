# [SYSTEM REPORT] OWS-003 Pass 7 — Structural System

**Target:** OWS-003 — Verdant Continuum Foods Cold-Chain Culture Nursery  
**Gate A:** PASSED r1  
**Status:** COMPLETE

## Structural concept

OWS-003 is an **older masonry/steel orchard cannery with a later insulated VCF cold-chain conversion inserted into it**. The structure should therefore show two construction generations rather than pretending the entire facility was built at once.

## Retained donor structure

### Main processing hall

The high brick hall remains the principal old-building frame.

Use:

- brick perimeter infill;
- repeated steel or concrete vertical frames at roughly 6-block rhythm;
- high transverse beams/roof ribs;
- the retained roof-light strips carried by real frames rather than floating on the roof plane;
- a clear high-volume interior capable of accepting later insulated cold-room modules.

The hall's broad walls are permitted only where the structural bay rhythm remains visible.

### Lower front/admin annex

The lower annex remains a heavier masonry/concrete support volume.

Use:

- load-bearing brick/concrete walls;
- smaller structural spans than the high hall;
- localized steel headers where VCF later opened wider controlled thresholds into operations.

## VCF conversion structure

VCF's cold rooms and nursery cells are later **box-within-box** installations.

They should read as:

- white/light-gray insulated room shells inside the older brick frame;
- independent service ceilings/headers where practical;
- controlled framed-glass observation panels only where operationally useful;
- clean thresholds supported by steel headers rather than arbitrary holes in masonry.

The conversion should not replace every old brick wall. The contrast between older shell and cleaner inserted volumes is part of the history.

## Hall structural grid

For the Gate-B model, use a working primary grid approximately aligned to:

- X = 25, 31, 37, 43, 49, 55;
- Z = 20, 26, 32, 38, 43.

Exact coordinates may shift by one block to preserve circulation, but cold-room walls, roof-light framing, plant supports and service trunks should respect this rhythm.

## Roof-light support

The three Gate-A roof-light strips must gain:

- side rails/steel framing;
- transverse support at major structural bays;
- visible relationship to the high hall below.

They are retained cannery architecture repurposed for monitored nursery/cold-chain operations, not decoration.

## Refrigeration-plant support

The roof plant must sit over the structural/service side of the conditioned hall.

Requirements:

- equipment groups land on or close to primary hall frames;
- plant deck/walkways bridge between supported points;
- vertical service risers descend near cold-vault/nursery service corridors;
- heavy equipment should not appear suspended over unsupported glazing or broad empty roof slabs.

## Maintenance tower

The Gate-A maintenance tower becomes a real structural/service core.

It requires:

- continuous vertical enclosure/frame;
- usable stair/ladder path;
- roof landing;
- direct access to plant deck;
- separation from freight circulation at ground level.

## Receiving structure

The east receiving threshold needs:

- two side jambs/columns;
- steel header;
- recessed insulated vestibule behind the exterior frame;
- canopy tied to the actual opening;
- protected loading apron.

## Dispatch structure

The rear/south dispatch threshold needs:

- distinct jamb/header system;
- canopy/loading frame tied to the outbound opening;
- room for conditioned staging immediately behind it;
- structural separation from the receiving system.

## Floor/load logic

Industrial/service floors should read differently from admin and cold-room floors:

- old factory floor or durable concrete in retained industrial circulation;
- cleaner light-gray/white floors inside insulated biological handling spaces;
- marked cold-chain staging lanes only at actual handoff points.

## Pass 7 decision

**STRUCTURAL SYSTEM: COMPLETE.**

Gate B must demonstrate that the old cannery frame, later insulated rooms, roof lights, refrigeration plant, receiving/dispatch portals and maintenance tower all form one believable supported building.
