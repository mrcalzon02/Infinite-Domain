# OWS-001 Pass 5 — Minecraft Scale Translation

## Decision

The existing **39 x 13 x 33** structure bounds are large enough for OWS-001's neighborhood function and are therefore retained for the first Gate-A massing study.

This is a deliberate scale decision, not donor preservation for its own sake.

A common neighborhood culture-service depot should not become a giant research campus merely because more blocks are available. The heavy rebuild will use the existing volume more intelligently and may reopen the dimension decision only if Gate A proves that the program cannot read cleanly.

## Coordinate convention for the massing study

These are planning bounds, not immutable final room coordinates.

- X: 0–38
- Y: 0–12
- Z: 0–32
- front/public approach: low Z
- rear/service approach: high Z

- roof/parapet around Y 8–9

This provides a one-storey service building with approximately 5–6 blocks of useful interior clear height depending on ceiling/service treatment.

## Human-scale dimensions

### Public circulation

- main entrance: 2 blocks wide minimum;
- vestibule clear path: 3–4 blocks where possible;
- customer queue: 3 blocks clear beside service positions where possible;
- waiting/information nook: compact, never a large empty lobby.

### Staff circulation

- ordinary service route: minimum 2 blocks clear;
- primary cold-chain/handling aisle: target 3 blocks clear;
- short controlled passages may narrow where doors/partitions require it, but no one-block maze should define routine operations.

### Freight

- rear freight/load opening: target 3–4 blocks wide;
- receiving staging: enough depth for at least two distinct crate/pallet positions plus staff passage;
- rear exterior apron: approximately 3–5 blocks of meaningful staging depth inside the structure's site bounds where geometry permits.

### Counters and workstations

- public issue/return counter depth: 1–2 blocks;
- staff working depth behind counter: minimum 2 blocks;
- inspection/wash bench cluster: 2–3 blocks deep;
- evidence/records station: small office/workpoint, not a 10-block treasure room.

### Cold storage

- equipment banks: 1–2 blocks deep depending on block choice;
- service aisle: 3 blocks preferred;
- opposite equipment or wall clearance must avoid an implausibly cramped one-block slot.

## Vertical scale

OWS-001 remains primarily one storey.

Recommended vertical reading:

- Y 1: primary finished floor datum;
- Y 2–6/7: occupied room volume;
- Y 7–8: ceiling/service zone depending on room;
- Y 8–9: roof/parapet composition;
- Y 9–12: selective mechanical/sign/plant elements only.

A uniform six-block-high empty hall across the entire footprint is not allowed. Ceiling height should vary modestly between:

- public pavilion;
- cold locker/service hall;
- rear receiving;
- offices/support rooms;
- roof/mechanical zones.

## Structural bay rhythm for Gate A

The facade and main body should use a loose bay rhythm around **5–6 blocks** rather than uninterrupted 30-block walls.

Gate A should be able to see:

- entrance bay hierarchy;
- at least one setback/projection on the public side;
- a return/service side volume;
- rear receiving articulation;
- roof plant placement;
- non-identical treatment of public and service elevations.

The rhythm must support later windows, columns, doors and interior partitions rather than existing only as facade decoration.

## Site-scale restraint

The depot should feel important because it was **ordinary**, not because it is enormous.

Therefore:

- no tower;
- no monumental atrium;
- no giant clear-span warehouse;
- no ceremonial campus plaza;
- no laboratory bunker silhouette.

The distinguishing features are controlled public service, cold-chain architecture, clean/dirty flow separation, visible service plant, and VCF's approachable biological-industrial identity.

## Gate-A massing acceptance criteria

The 39 x 13 x 33 dimensions survive only if the massing preview demonstrates all of the following:

1. the structure no longer reads primarily as the grocery donor;
2. the front entrance is clearly the public entrance;
3. rear freight/service access is distinguishable without signage;
4. return-side and clean-cold-side functions can plausibly occupy different volumes;
5. the roof mechanical zone feels intentionally composed;
6. there is enough facade depth and roof variation to avoid a decorated rectangle;
7. the building remains believable as common neighborhood infrastructure rather than an oversized institution;
8. planned hero spaces have enough volume to matter without creating dead empty space.

If Gate A fails one of these macro conditions, dimensions or massing are reopened before structural/interior detailing begins.
