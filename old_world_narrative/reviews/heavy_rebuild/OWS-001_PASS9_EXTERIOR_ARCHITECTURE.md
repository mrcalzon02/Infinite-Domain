# OWS-001 Pass 9 — Exterior Architecture

## Intent

OWS-001 should look like **ordinary neighborhood infrastructure deliberately standardized by Verdant Continuum Foods**, not a grocery store with a corporate stripe and not a biotech fortress.

Gate-A r2 massing remains authoritative for the D0 exterior envelope. Pass 9 gives each elevation an architectural job tied to the rooms behind it.

## Front / public elevation

### Material hierarchy

- durable masonry or stone-brick base/body retained as neighborhood context;
- white washable VCF cladding around the entrance pavilion and service identity field;
- green used as a controlled corporate accent/blade, not full-surface paint;
- framed glass concentrated around public service spaces.

### Entrance composition

The approved narrow canopy remains centered on the double entry.

Required refinement:

- supported canopy edges remain visible;
- double entry sits inside a clearly framed central bay;
- glazing flanks the entrance without turning the entire facade into a curtain wall;
- wall depth or pilasters break the facade into 5–6 block structural bays;
- the full company/facility identity has a designated sign field above/beside the public threshold.

### Window logic

Front glazing corresponds to:

- vestibule / customer orientation;
- public issue/return interface;
- optional partial view toward the culture-locker hero space.

No public glazing should reveal freight receiving, sanitation, or dirty-return storage as if they were retail displays.

## West / return-service elevation

The west annex should communicate **controlled handling**, not public retail.

Required elements:

- lower mass retained from Gate A;
- major service-bay frame becomes a real staff/service threshold;
- fewer, smaller process windows than the public facade;
- washable white/light-gray service material with masonry context;
- space for operational return/sanitation markers later;
- no D1 yellow crisis overlay yet.

The architecture must make it believable that returned biological material enters and is handled here without advertising the side as a public entrance.

## East / cold-chain service elevation

This is the clean technical side.

Required elements:

- taller insulated white/light-gray mass;
- limited glazing;
- controlled maintenance/service door aligned to the approved service frame;
- visible vertical service/riser relationship toward rooftop refrigeration;
- parapet/roof edge able to support partial equipment screening;
- no warehouse loading-door language.

The player should read this side as equipment-supported cold infrastructure even before seeing the interior.

## Rear / freight-service elevation

The rear elevation is deliberately more industrial.

Required elements:

- cinder/industrial wall field;
- steel-framed 3–4 block freight opening;
- canopy tied exactly to the bay;
- factory-floor/apron transition at the threshold;
- adjacent wall bays remain solid and structurally convincing;
- lower supervisor/records bump-out uses modest controlled glazing rather than loading architecture.

The rear must not resemble a second customer entrance.

## Roof architecture

The roof is part of the facade system because it is visible in the mandatory elevated review.

Required D0 roof logic:

- front workplace roof, lower rear-process roof, annex roof and taller cold-chain roof remain visibly distinct;
- local parapets or edge bands where functionally useful;
- four-part refrigeration equipment cluster retained as the basis for real plant;
- maintenance spine preserved between equipment units;
- partial screens attached to real roof/support lines;
- one staff/service vertical access point reaches the plant;
- later utility pipes/conduit may cross roofs only when their destinations are clear.

Avoid one continuous decorative parapet that erases the Gate-A stepped silhouette.

## Drainage / weather logic

Minecraft does not require a full building-services simulation, but the exterior should not imply impossible water handling.

- flat/low-slope roofs may use parapets with selected drain/downspout positions;
- downspouts/service pipes should prefer non-public corners or service elevations;
- the entrance canopy should shed away from the doorway in visual logic;
- roof equipment should not block the only plausible drainage path.

## Site edges

Retain Gate-A r2 distinction:

- front arrival hardscape;
- compact building/service walk;
- rear freight apron;
- east/west service strips;
- softer perimeter beyond those functional surfaces.

Later D3 decay may break these surfaces, but D0 should look intentionally maintained.

## Exterior rejection conditions

Pass 9 fails if:

- the building can again be described as `supermarket plus green stripe`;
- green concrete carries more identity than shape/material/sign hierarchy;
- every elevation uses the same window rhythm;
- public facade glazing exposes illogical rooms;
- the rear freight bay looks like a front entrance;
- east/west service frames have no related door/utility function;
- rooftop plant feels detached from the cold-chain volume;
- blank side walls survive where Gate A explicitly required service rhythm;
- a decorative facade pattern conflicts with the room plan behind it.

## Pass 9 result

**EXTERIOR ARCHITECTURE: DEFINED FOR D0.**

Final implementation must be reviewed again at Gate B with the actual intact room plan and equipment behind these elevations.
