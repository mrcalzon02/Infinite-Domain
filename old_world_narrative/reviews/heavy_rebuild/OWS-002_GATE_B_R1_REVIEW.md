## Review basis

This review uses the persisted fixed-camera artifact set at:

`old_world_narrative/reviews/heavy_rebuild/visual/OWS-002/gate_b_intact/r1/`

The r1 builder passed its executable D0 assertions before rendering. Those assertions prove that the public queue, controlled staff doors, grow aisles, east service strip, harvest transfers, relief dispatch lane, roof ladder/trapdoor, crop density, irrigation density and twelve purpose-driven signs exist mechanically.

That mechanical success does **not** satisfy Visual Gate B by itself. The authoritative doctrine requires the intact facility to look capable of performing its function, to communicate institutional identity without relying entirely on signs/logos, to distinguish front-of-house from back-of-house, and to have facade/roof/structural systems that agree with the rooms behind them.

## What r1 succeeds at

- The Gate-A r2 massing survives the intact fit-out. The grow hall and roof lantern remain the hero volume.
- The public north entrance is legible and visibly different from the service thresholds.
- The interior cutaway clearly shows three repeated two-tier cultivation banks rather than decorative isolated crop props.
- The hall has visible structural portal frames and high service infrastructure.
- The west support wing reads as a separate lower civic/support volume.
- The north public/admin zone and the high cultivation hall are spatially distinct.
- The code provides a real public -> controlled staff -> grow-operations sequence.
- East receiving/batch check, clean culture stock, nutrient/irrigation support and localized quality hold are physically separated.
- Raw harvest can move west to wash/check and checked product can return to packing/dispatch.
- The south dispatch lane remains deliberately clear.
- Roof maintenance access survives intact-state reconstruction.
- Sign placement is support-verified rather than floating or decorative.

These are important improvements over the baseline and establish a valid operational skeleton.

## Blocking visual findings

### 1. Tall grow-hall elevations are still too blank

The fixed exterior views show very large uninterrupted brick surfaces on the high hall. The building contains dense cultivation, structural frames and overhead irrigation, yet the exterior walls reveal almost none of that rhythm.

This violates the intact-state requirement that facade, structural and room systems agree with one another. It also leaves obvious primitive construction visible at building/facade scale.

**Required correction:** express the interior bay rhythm externally through aligned pilasters/frames, clerestory glazing, service panels or other credible adaptive-reuse articulation. Do not solve this with random facade garnish.

### 2. VCF identity still depends too heavily on the north entrance and signs

The public entrance has the strongest white/lime identity. From rear and side approaches the site still reads primarily as a brick community building with a greenhouse-like roof lantern rather than an emergency VCF food-production conversion.

The doctrine explicitly requires corporate identity to alter architecture itself rather than merely logo/palette/signage.

**Required correction:** extend VCF's clean biological-production language into the hall envelope, glazing/service rhythm and visible cultivation-support infrastructure while preserving the civic donor underneath.

### 3. East receiving remains visually underdeveloped as back-of-house architecture

The receiving threshold works mechanically, but the exterior review still reads the canopy/frame as a relatively thin appendage. The apron, recessed opening, batch-check function and clean-stock handoff are not yet strongly expressed as a service bay.

**Required correction:** integrate the receiving bay with the hall wall using a complete service portal, loading/inspection apron language and facade rhythm tied to the interior receiving zone.

### 4. South relief dispatch is functional but not yet a convincing high-throughput handoff

The central dispatch doors and clear internal lane exist, but the exterior canopy is visually isolated. The south face needs to show that relief goods moved through this threshold in volume.

**Required correction:** strengthen the opening-tied frame, route marking and wall/canopy relationship without turning this neighborhood site into a warehouse truck terminal.

### 5. Roof service does not visibly connect strongly enough to the cultivation system below

The roof plant is visible, but the intact exterior does not yet make the relationship between plant and grow hall particularly legible.

**Required correction:** add restrained visible service/riser/duct or pipe continuity between roof equipment and the cultivation/service side of the hall. Keep the roof lantern dominant.

### 6. Front-of-house/back-of-house distinction is stronger in code than in the rendered architecture

The public north face is reasonably legible, but the other facades do not yet communicate receiving, cultivation service, harvest handling and dispatch strongly enough through architectural form.

**Required correction:** make each service face explain what happens behind it using real thresholds, windows/clerestories, structure and utility cues rather than only signs.

## Non-blocking observations

- The current 51 x 21 x 47 envelope remains credible. No scale expansion is required by r1 findings.
- The grow-hall rack count and aisle spacing appear appropriate for this neighborhood emergency-conversion scale.
- The roof lantern remains worth retaining and should not be subordinated by additional rooftop equipment.
- The public/admin bar should remain visibly civic rather than becoming a generic biotech laboratory frontage.
- Historical anomaly, collapse damage, spawners, quest proof and centuries-later decay remain intentionally absent at this gate.
