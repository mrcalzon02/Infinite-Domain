## 1. Overall visual reading

The structure presently reads first as an **orchard plus brick cannery/packing plant** and only secondarily as anything connected to VCF cold-chain culture logistics.

That donor identity is not inherently a problem. In fact, it is useful adaptive-reuse history: a large agricultural company taking over an older processing site is plausible. The problem is that the VCF program has not transformed enough of the building to make the new use legible.

The present result is visually close to:

> **orchard cannery + thin corporate bands + one refrigerated glass insert**

rather than a complete cold-chain culture nursery.

## 2. Massing worth retaining provisionally

### Main brick processing hall — RETAIN AS RAW ARCHITECTURAL MASS

The high central/rear brick hall is useful.

It gives OWS-003:

- a credible agricultural-industrial scale;
- enough volume for cold vaults, nursery chambers and service infrastructure;
- a stronger silhouette than a purpose-built low warehouse would necessarily provide;
- visible adaptive-reuse history.

The hall should not remain an unexplained empty shell. Its retained mass must acquire a clear cold-chain structural and service logic.

### Lower front/side annex — RETAIN PROVISIONALLY

The lower white/brick wing can plausibly become some combination of:

- staff/public administrative threshold;
- batch records/licensing control;
- inspection / release offices;
- staff support;
- small quality-control rooms.

Its present architectural treatment is too generic, but its height contrast is useful.

### Long roof-light rhythm — RETAIN/REINTERPRET

The repeated long roof bands/skylight strips are one of the donor's most distinctive visual features.

They can support the conversion if they become functionally tied to:

- inspected nursery zones;
- service corridors;
- maintenance-access rhythm;
- environmental monitoring;
- clearly differentiated roof plant.

They should not remain decorative roof stripes.

### Orchard relationship — RETAIN AS SITE HISTORY, NOT AS THE FACILITY'S MAIN IDENTITY

The orchard rows occupy a major portion of the site and dominate several approach views.

That is valuable evidence that this was once an agricultural processing property. However, the orchard currently competes with the cold-chain building for visual priority.

The rebuild should preserve enough orchard structure to communicate the site's prior use while making the converted plant itself the clear architectural subject.

## 3. Exterior deficiencies

### VCF identity is too shallow

The existing facade treatment is mostly color bands and a few cyan/green accents.

There is no strong architectural distinction between:

- receiving;
- cold-chain service;
- staff/admin;
- nursery operations;
- outbound dispatch.

A player cannot currently identify the building's process from the exterior.

### Service thresholds are weak

No loading/receiving face reads as a serious temperature-controlled inbound threshold.

No dispatch face reads as an organized culture-shipping handoff.

There is no visible relationship between loading areas and internal cold storage.

### Refrigeration/environmental plant is absent as architecture

A cold-chain nursery of this scale requires visible support infrastructure.

The baseline roof has strong raw area and a useful repeated rhythm, but no convincing refrigeration plant, condenser field, service piping, maintenance access or environmental-control hierarchy explains the interior cooler program.

### Broad brick faces remain under-articulated

The largest elevations retain generic cannery-wall behavior. They need structural bay rhythm, cold-room/service insertions, loading architecture, controlled openings and utility logic rather than cosmetic surface noise.

## 4. Interior deficiencies

The baseline cutaway makes the central weakness especially clear.

A small framed-glass/cyan nursery enclosure is visually obvious, but it occupies only a limited portion of a much larger building. The surrounding rooms remain broad, simplified donor spaces with little evidence of a complete cold-chain workflow.

The current interior does not yet make the following sequence reconstructable:

`receiving -> batch intake -> temperature / seal inspection -> cold holding -> dormancy nursery -> release / licensing records -> packing -> dispatch`

Specific problems include:

- no convincing dedicated receiving/check zone;
- no large cold vault system proportionate to the building;
- nursery cells are too few and too isolated from building-wide service infrastructure;
- no strong batch-control / licensing-records node;
- no clear clean-stock versus suspect/quality-hold separation;
- no legible packing/dispatch preparation program;
- no meaningful staff/service circulation system;
- no convincing vertical or roof maintenance path despite the 24-block structure height.

The generated structural metric reporting **vertical access span = 0** agrees with the visual review: the current site does not communicate maintainable multi-level/roof service access.

## 5. Proof-location review

The canonical proof chest remains functionally protected as a requirement, but its current coordinate is not granted architectural approval merely because it already exists.

The final proof should belong in a plausible decision/records location associated with:

- batch release;
- cold-chain exception handling;
- licensing destination;
- shipping authorization;
- or supervisor records.

The deterministic loot table and guaranteed acquisition contract must survive any final relocation.

## 6. Historical-layering implications

OWS-003 is an **Early anomaly** site.

The baseline contains no convincing reason to jump directly to catastrophic containment imagery.

The later historical pass should therefore remain restrained:

- gasket or seal inspection;
- localized quality hold;
- rerouted batches;
- replacement seal stock;
- refrigeration maintenance pressure;
- one or more suspect nursery/cold-room cells;
- temporary batch routing changes.

The site's eventual D3 decay can be substantial enough to read as centuries-old abandonment, but the pre-collapse story should still be one of a globally functioning system encountering a material reliability problem—not a late-war quarantine fortress.
