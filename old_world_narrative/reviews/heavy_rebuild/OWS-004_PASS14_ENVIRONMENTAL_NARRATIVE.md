## Narrative objective

The structure must communicate the central OWS-004 fact before the player reads any book:

**VCF succeeded at making an extraordinarily resilient urban food-production system, and the crop remained productive even as the machinery and institution around it began to fail.**

The canonical OWS-004 loot already supports both ends of that story. Dr. Mara Vey's early record celebrates EP-7/PT-9 resilience after nutrient interruption and imagines farms operating without normal soil/weather constraints. The later technician log reports failing coolant seals, peeling insulation, unstable ventilation, heavy maintenance backlog and doubled ration demand while the fungus remains healthy; it also records Grey Bloom reappearing around the inspection booth after being painted over.

Environmental evidence must therefore establish those ideas physically rather than requiring the player to infer them only from transcripts.

## D0 environmental evidence — success and normalization

D0 should visibly communicate:

- **commercial confidence:** the public/demo podium and greenhouse crown present cultivation technology as something to show visitors, not hide from them;
- **high throughput:** receiving, vertical material transfer, repeated cultivation floors, harvest consolidation and dispatch all have enough capacity to look like city-scale food infrastructure;
- **resilience engineering:** each production module has redundant-looking environmental/water distribution and local inspection capability rather than being dependent on one decorative pipe;
- **batch discipline:** floor-local work/inspection nodes, routing signs and harvest handoffs imply standardized production lots;
- **urban integration:** the building is compact and vertical because land is valuable, while industrial logistics remain concentrated in the podium/core;
- **VCF optimism:** clean white/lime/light-blue institutional language, viewing areas and professional wayfinding remain visible without making the tower a theme park.

The player should understand why people trusted this system.

## D1 environmental evidence — containment without admitting systemic failure

D1 must communicate an organization trying to treat a dangerous observation as a bounded operating exception.

Required physical evidence:

1. **Upper-floor isolation uses existing architecture.** Seal tape/barrier analogues, temporary inspection markers and restricted-entry signs appear at the upper staff/freight thresholds and environmental shutoff, visibly layered over the clean D0 system.
2. **Lower-floor production remains ordinary.** Grow banks, harvest handoffs and service headers on cultivation floors 1–3 remain active and comparatively clean.
3. **Maintenance backlog is visible but localized.** Spare seals, replacement pipe/duct components, maintenance crates and tagged failed parts accumulate near service-control nodes rather than being scattered as decorative junk.
4. **Ventilation concern has a physical locus.** The upper environmental branch and inspection/return-air side receive the strongest temporary control treatment.
5. **Grey Bloom evidence is limited and contradictory.** One inspection/control surface near the isolated module should show an attempted clean/paint-over zone with renewed grey/fungal occupation nearby; do not cover the whole tower with growth.
6. **Ration-pressure logistics are visible below.** Dispatch/pallet staging can be fuller than D0, implying demand pressure while the facility continues shipping from unaffected production.
7. **Security/inspection pressure belongs at outbound control, not everywhere.** A reinforced pallet-inspection/check position may appear near dispatch without converting the whole tower into a military base.

The contradiction should be legible: the organism/crop is robust; the engineered systems and institutional certainty are becoming fragile.

## D3 environmental evidence — civilization beneath the ruin

D3 must preserve evidence from both D0 and D1 under centuries of decay.

The strongest storytelling relationships should be causal pairs:

- failed crown glazing above wet/damaged upper production areas;
- broken environmental riser segment with disconnected-but-recognizable floor branch below;
- upper containment seals/signage surviving beside later wall/glazing loss;
- old replacement parts/maintenance staging near service nodes now partially buried or weathered;
- lower-floor grow banks surviving better than the exposed upper module;
- dispatch/ration-pressure staging surviving as pallets/crates/markings at the actual outbound route;
- attempted Grey Bloom cleanup evidence persisting at a real inspection/control point;
- public/demo architecture remaining unexpectedly legible beneath the ruin, emphasizing how ordinary and celebrated the system once was.

## Non-text narrative facts required at Gate C

A player looking only at the geometry/props should be able to infer at least the following:

1. this was a vertical food-production tower, not an office;
2. cultivation depended on shared building-scale environmental and nutrient systems;
3. people and freight used different vertical routes;
4. one upper production level was deliberately isolated after normal operation had already been established;
5. lower production and shipping continued after that isolation;
6. later tower failure followed building-service/weather breakdown rather than a single destructive event;
7. the resilient crop/system outlasted some of the machinery designed to support it.

## Text/evidence relationship

The canonical proof item `kubejs:evercrop_cultivation_handbook` and the two written/audio-transcript records should **confirm and deepen** the above physical story. They must not be the only source of the story.

The early Vey transcript should feel like an explanation for why the confident D0 tower exists. The later technician transcript should feel like recognition of what the player has already seen: failing seals/ventilation/maintenance capacity alongside continued production and increasingly desperate outbound demand.
