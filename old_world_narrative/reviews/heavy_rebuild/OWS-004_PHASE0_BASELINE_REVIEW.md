## Donor / shipping audit

The exact shipping baseline was inspected from the persisted fixed-camera images, roof/top review and interior cutaway. The structure is mechanically substantial but architecturally still dominated by its ruined-office-tower donor.

Useful inherited bones are limited to the tall multi-story form, repeated floor plates, podium/tower relationship, vertical-access opportunity and roof area. These are appropriate starting conditions for a vertical agricultural facility, but they do not presently communicate that use.

## Exterior visual findings

The baseline is a blunt dark rectangular office tower on a similarly blunt podium. Sparse cyan glazing and repeated white/red external stair/balcony elements read as generic office or apartment appendages. The agricultural program is almost entirely hidden from exterior review.

Blocking findings:

1. **No agricultural silhouette.** The tower has no visible cultivation-floor hierarchy, greenhouse crown, service spine or production-oriented floor rhythm.
2. **The podium is fortress-like and generic.** It does not communicate public demonstration, receiving, harvest handling, packing or urban food-production optimism.
3. **The rooftop element does not read as a greenhouse.** It appears as a small box/patch rather than a luminous cultivation or environmental-control crown.
4. **Facade identity is applied, not architectural.** Green bands cannot overcome the underlying office-tower reading.
5. **Cultivation floors are not externally legible.** Window spacing and facade rhythm do not explain repeated controlled-environment production floors.
6. **No believable agricultural utility anatomy is visible.** Nutrient/water/air risers, maintenance access and roof-to-floor service relationships are not expressed as a coherent system.
7. **Service logistics are weak.** Receiving, material lift, harvest transfer and packing/dispatch thresholds are not convincingly distinguished.
8. **External stairs/balconies read as donor leftovers.** They do not currently explain emergency egress, maintenance or production service.
9. **Public-facing VCF optimism is absent.** The building looks defensive and industrially anonymous rather than like a highly promoted urban food-production institution.
10. **Active-containment history cannot be read architecturally.** The upper-floor anomaly exists mechanically but the building provides no convincing isolation/containment anatomy from the baseline review.

## Interior / sectional findings

The cutaway confirms that functional implementation exists: multiple cultivation levels, grow rows, equipment and a long vertical stack. That is useful coverage, but the repeated floors still read as equipment inserted into generic office slabs.

The rebuild must convert the floor stack into an intentional production sequence with an understandable public/service/production hierarchy, vertical material flow, utilities, harvesting/packing logic and a credible upper containment zone.

## Retain / rebuild decision

Retain provisionally:

- overall high-rise verticality;
- useful structural/floor repetition;
- vertical-access/core potential;
- broad podium/tower relationship;
- roof area as the location for greenhouse/environmental plant.

Rebuild substantially:

- podium and public threshold;
- facade bay rhythm and cultivation-floor expression;
- rooftop greenhouse/crown;
- utility/service risers and maintenance expression;
- receiving, vertical freight, harvest and dispatch architecture;
- generic exterior appendages;
- upper-floor containment architecture;
- institutional identity throughout.

The objective is not to disguise the office tower with more green. It is to make the inherited high-rise bones plausibly function as an industrial mycological vertical farm.
