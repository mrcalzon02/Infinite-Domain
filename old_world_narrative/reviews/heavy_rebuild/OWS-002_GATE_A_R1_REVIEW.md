# [SYSTEM REPORT] OWS-002 Gate A r1 — Massing Review

**Target:** OWS-002 — Verdant Continuum Foods Emergency Community Grow Hall  
**Gate:** A — D0 massing / site interpretation  
**Fixed camera set:** `ows002_fixed_v1`  
**Artifact:** `old_world_narrative/reviews/heavy_rebuild/visual/OWS-002/gate_a_massing/r1/review_manifest.json`  
**Decision:** **REVISION REQUIRED**

## Review scope

Gate A judges only macro architecture: retained civic massing, hall hierarchy, public arrival, service receiving, relief dispatch, roof lantern, roof/service plant and site occupation. Crop racks, machinery, detailed rooms, final signage, anomaly dressing, loot, rubble and microdetail are intentionally absent.

The r1 fixed-camera set was reviewed against the Phase-0 baseline, Passes 2–5, the donor-source geometry and VCF institutional language.

## What r1 improved

- The community-center origin remains recognizable rather than being replaced by a generic industrial warehouse.
- The projecting north lobby remains a clear public reference point.
- The grow-hall volume and glazed lantern survive as distinct architecture.
- Public, east-service and south-dispatch hardscape are no longer conceptually one route.
- Receiving and dispatch are attached to real donor thresholds rather than arbitrary new doors.
- The west/rear roof-service location remains subordinate to the lantern and preserves the donor maintenance concept.

These ideas should survive r2.

## Blocking finding 1 — lower civic mass is too dominant

The r1 lower community shell runs nearly the full building footprint to Y=14. From multiple exterior angles it reads as one huge rectangular civic/institutional box with a slightly taller hall added inside/behind it.

This is not enough hierarchy for the approved functional definition. The grow hall should be the hero volume, while the retained civic rooms should read as a lower L-shaped support/administrative wrap.

**Required correction:** rebuild the lower mass as explicit lower west/rear civic support plus a north administrative/public crossbar instead of one nearly hall-height full-footprint shell.

## Blocking finding 2 — grow-hall / lantern hierarchy is too weak

The hall reaches Y=18 and the lantern reaches the top of the template, but because the surrounding lower body reaches Y=14, the height difference is visually modest. The hall does not dominate the silhouette as strongly as the emergency cultivation program requires.

**Required correction:** lower and step the civic wings while retaining the 51 x 21 x 47 envelope. Keep the hall at the existing tall scale so its lantern becomes unmistakably dominant.

## Blocking finding 3 — east receiving reads as an attachment, not a service threshold

The east receiving canopy/apron is correctly located but its thin roof and limited framing make it read as a shelf attached to the hall wall in the opposing-angle review.

**Required correction:** use a compact but complete receiving bay frame: vertical side members, a real header/canopy tied to the wall opening, a recessed threshold and a small service/identity mass that belongs to the civic scale.

Do not turn it into a warehouse truck dock.

## Blocking finding 4 — south dispatch needs stronger bay geometry

The south dispatch canopy is tied to the correct donor exit and its scale is broadly appropriate, but the broad thin slab and sparse supports do not yet read as a deliberate emergency relief handoff.

**Required correction:** narrow the canopy to the actual dispatch opening and give it a clear structural frame / landing relationship. Keep the south apron distinct and retain emergency-yellow routing only as macro wayfinding.

## Blocking finding 5 — roof service is partially lost in the oversized base mass

The separated service units are better than a single rooftop bunker, but the r1 west/rear roof-service composition is visually less legible than intended because the surrounding civic roof is too high and broad.

**Required correction:** after lowering/stepping the civic wing, keep a compact cluster of separated service units with maintenance gaps and a visible relationship to the retained ladder/roof access.

## Blocking finding 6 — adaptive-reuse story needs clearer exterior layering

The r1 colors distinguish VCF/emergency insertions, but the macro geometry still reads more like a modified new building than a civic shell with targeted conversion layers.

**Required correction:** r2 should make three generations of massing readable without detailed signage:

1. original low civic support wings;
2. original tall hall and lantern;
3. compact emergency/VCF additions at the north, east, south and roof-service points.

## Required r2 invariants

r2 must retain:

- `51 x 21 x 47` envelope provisionally;
- north civic entrance and projecting lobby;
- tall east/rear grow hall;
- roof lantern;
- east receiving location;
- south dispatch location;
- west/rear wet-service/support relationship;
- east/rear roof maintenance route;
- differentiated public/service/dispatch site zones.

r2 must still contain **no crop racks, interior machinery, final readable signage, D1 isolation, D3 damage, proof chest or microdetail**.

## Gate-A r1 decision

**REVISION REQUIRED.**

OWS-002 remains the sole active heavy-rebuild target. Structural-system, circulation, exterior/interior detail, operational systems and all later gates remain blocked until a revised D0 massing study passes Gate A.
