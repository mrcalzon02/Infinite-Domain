# OWS-001 Pass 10 — Interior Architecture

## Intent

The D0 interior must make the building's function obvious through room sequence, partitions, floor/ceiling treatment, visibility, and work-space proportions **before machinery props or crisis damage are relied upon**.

The approved 39 x 13 x 33 envelope remains in force.

## Room plan

Coordinates below are working interior targets and may move one block to align with structural frames during implementation.

### 1. Vestibule / customer orientation

Approximate zone:

- X 15–23
- Z 4–8

Architecture:

- front double entrance;
- glazed/public finish;
- direct visual split toward issue and returns;
- ceiling lower than the cold-service volume so the room feels welcoming rather than industrial;
- no unrestricted opening into staff areas.

### 2. Return service counter

Approximate zone:

- public side X 13–17, Z 8–11
- staff transfer immediately behind toward west

Architecture:

- solid counter handoff;
- partial glazing/screening acceptable;
- back opening points directly into return-processing route;
- visually distinct from issue but part of the same calm service pavilion.

### 3. Culture issue / account counter

Approximate zone:

- public side X 20–24, Z 8–11

Architecture:

- 2–3 service positions implied by counter rhythm;
- staff work depth behind;
- immediate relationship to issue staging and locker-service area;
- customer cannot step behind the counter without using a staff door.

### 4. Culture-locker / issue hero space

Approximate zone:

- X 17–30
- Z 11–19

Architecture:

- repeated cold-bank wall or island rhythm;
- service aisle at least 2 blocks, target 3;
- public can read the equipment from the service interface without wandering inside it;
- slightly taller/technical ceiling than the pavilion;
- clear route to clean holding and issue staging.

This is the primary interior hero space for a common VCF site.

### 5. Clean issue staging

Approximate zone:

- X 18–24
- Z 13–18, integrated behind the issue counter/locker interface

Architecture:

- compact workbench/depot zone;
- clear surfaces for order assembly;
- physically on the clean side of the building;
- no shared dirty-return work surface.

### 6. Return sanitation / inspection

Approximate zone:

- X 4–11
- Z 14–20

Architecture:

- enclosed or screened controlled room;
- durable cleanable floor/walls;
- wet/wash side and inspection/workbench side;
- staff route can proceed rearward after inspection;
- service-side opening aligned to the west service frame where useful.

### 7. Quality-hold room

Approximate zone:

- X 4–11
- Z 20–24

D0 architecture:

- permanent normal controlled room;
- enclosed threshold;
- one or two rack/crate positions;
- no yellow emergency floor field yet;
- designed so D1 can visibly add temporary isolation without rebuilding the room.

### 8. Returned-crate consolidation

Approximate zone:

- X 10–16
- Z 21–27

Architecture:

- pallet/crate-capable floor area;
- direct connection from sanitation;
- direct or short connection to rear dispatch;
- visually and physically separated from clean cold stock.

### 9. Receiving / batch-temperature check

Approximate zone:

- X 14–22
- Z 24–30

Architecture:

- freight bay opens here;
- receiving staging immediately inside;
- inspection/check station before any clean stock door;
- durable floor and higher service ceiling;
- clear staff route around staged pallets.

### 10. Clean cold holding

Approximate zone:

- X 24–33
- Z 18–24

Architecture:

- insulated/technical enclosure;
- limited glazing except staff observation where useful;
- repeated cold-storage geometry;
- direct relationship to batch check and locker/issue service;
- vertical relationship to roof refrigeration plant.

### 11. Supervisor / batch records

Approximate zone:

- X 25–32
- Z 25–29

Architecture:

- small lower-ceiling office/workpoint;
- service-side window or interior observation opening where useful;
- desk/counter/records wall;
- radio/communications point;
- deterministic evidence chest belongs here in the eventual authoritative implementation.

### 12. Staff/service support

Use remaining structurally sensible small spaces for:

- cleaning supply cupboard;
- staff locker nook;
- compact washroom/break alcove if geometry allows.

No large decorative staff lounge is required.

## Partition logic

Preferred interior partitions:

- masonry/cinder for wet/service/control rooms;
- white/light-gray clean wall fields for cold/public support;
- framed glass only where observation/visibility is operationally useful;
- counters create handoff boundaries in the pavilion;
- doors correspond to circulation hierarchy rather than arbitrary room subdivision.

## Floor language

### Public

- smooth stone / light clean finish;
- subtle VCF accent only at wayfinding/thresholds.

### Clean cold-chain / issue support

- light-gray or washable industrial finish;
- enough contrast to distinguish cold/service aisles.

### Return sanitation

- durable washable surface;
- drainage/wet-area visual cues can be added in Pass 11 without turning the room into a sewer.

### Receiving / rear process

- factory-floor / industrial service surface.

### Office/support

- simpler clean finished floor.

## Ceiling and lighting zones

### Pavilion

- lower finished ceiling;
- regular calm lighting.

### Culture-locker/issue space

- higher semi-technical ceiling;
- visible but organized service/lighting rhythm.

### Sanitation / rear process

- utilitarian ceiling and exposed/boxed service where appropriate.

### Clean cold hold

- controlled technical ceiling with visible relationship to refrigeration support.

No room should be a seven-block-high empty cube simply because the shell allows it.

## Sightline logic

From the main entrance, a player should be able to understand:

- where issue happens;
- where returns happen;
- that a controlled cold-storage operation exists behind the service interface.

The player should **not** immediately see every back-of-house room from the door.

From receiving, staff should understand:

- where batch check occurs;
- which direction is clean holding;
- where the supervisor/records station is.

From sanitation, staff should understand:

- where normal accepted returns go;
- where quality-hold material goes.

## Interior rejection conditions

Pass 10 fails if:

- rooms are distinguished only by floor color;
- large empty volumes survive without a function;
- cold lockers are exposed as retail shelving;
- public counters lack staff working depth;
- sanitation and quality hold are one undifferentiated cage;
- evidence room is a detached treasure closet;
- ceiling heights ignore room function;
- window placement from Pass 9 disagrees with the rooms behind it;
- interior circulation breaks the clean/dirty separation defined in Pass 8.

## Pass 10 result

**INTERIOR ARCHITECTURE: DEFINED FOR D0.**

The next pass will populate these rooms with operational systems that complete the workflows rather than merely decorate them.
