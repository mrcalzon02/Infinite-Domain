# [SYSTEM REPORT] OWS-004 Pass 18 — Causal Damage and Decay

**Target:** OWS-004 — Verdant Continuum Foods Mycological Vertical Farm Tower  
**Status:** COMPLETE — planning only until Gate-C implementation  
**Intact baseline:** Gate-B r4 PASSED

## Damage doctrine

OWS-004 must decay like a tall controlled-environment agricultural building whose crown, glazing, vertical utilities and exposed loading edges were abandoned for centuries.

Its active-containment episode does **not** destroy the tower. D1 is a localized upper-floor operating intervention. D3 is the later worldgen ruin caused by long-term loss of maintenance, weather entry, service failure and ordinary structural exposure.

Damage therefore follows systems and exposure. It must never become uniform random block deletion.

## D1 containment overlay

D1 changes are intentionally small compared with D3.

### Cultivation 04 staff threshold

- retain the intact doorway and vestibule geometry;
- add temporary yellow exception/seal coding around the controlled threshold;
- add a bounded barrier/seal layer that clearly looks retrofitted onto the existing architecture;
- preserve the staff stair and landing on the safe side of the threshold.

### Cultivation 04 freight/service threshold

- add a second controlled barrier at the upper freight/material handoff;
- retain the lower-floor freight core in ordinary operation;
- use inspection/hold staging rather than rubble.

### Environmental branch 04

- show the existing local shutoff as deliberately isolated/locked out;
- add limited maintenance/replacement stock nearby;
- preserve all lower-floor environmental branches intact.

### Upper production banks

- place one or two suspect grow-bank groups into bounded hold/inspection condition;
- keep other upper-floor architecture readable;
- redirect ordinary harvest handoff into hold/return staging;
- do not spread contamination markings across the lower floors.

### Dispatch pressure

- add modest extra pallet/crate staging on the real outbound route to imply continued lower-floor production and rising demand;
- preserve aisle widths and door operation.

## D3 causal damage map

### 1. Greenhouse / environmental crown — highest damage priority

The crown is the most exposed and service-dependent portion of the tower.

D3 should:

- remove selected greenhouse glazing panels, especially on exposed corners/edges;
- retain enough glass/frame rhythm that the crown remains recognizable as cultivation architecture;
- break or displace one environmental equipment group;
- interrupt one roof service header/pipe branch;
- weather one maintenance edge/catwalk zone;
- create localized roof penetrations/wet paths beneath failed glazing or service points rather than an arbitrary crater.

Do **not** remove the entire greenhouse or flatten the crown.

### 2. Upper cultivation / former containment floor

This floor receives the strongest interior production damage because it combines earlier isolation with later crown/service exposure.

D3 should:

- preserve the old containment threshold, yellow coding and quality-hold geometry as archaeological evidence;
- fail one controlled door/panel edge sufficiently to permit exploration without destructive excavation;
- damage selected grow banks beneath roof/wet-service paths;
- break one environmental branch segment downstream of the historical isolation point;
- preserve the main aisle and enough repeated rack geometry to identify Cultivation 04 as one of the standardized production floors.

The old containment system should look obsolete and weathered, not actively powered.

### 3. Lower cultivation floors

Damage becomes less severe and more localized downward.

Requirements:

- retain at least two clearly legible lower cultivation-bank groups on every lower floor;
- keep principal aisles traversable;
- use localized missing scaffolding/rack blocks beneath vertical wet/service paths;
- allow limited floor-edge glazing loss where exterior exposure makes sense;
- preserve harvest handoff and service-header logic on enough floors to reconstruct the repeated production system.

Do not apply the same damage mask to all floors.

### 4. East vertical service core

The service core should explain much of the tower's long-term failure.

D3 should:

- break one major upper environmental/water riser segment;
- leave adjacent lower riser sections intact enough to show continuity;
- weather exposed service openings/landings;
- retain freight/material-transfer casing as a distinct system from the staff stair;
- preserve the r4 principal staff stair as a traversable exploration route;
- keep the west secondary-egress ladder recognizable.

No random core collapse may erase the distinction among people, freight and utilities.

### 5. Receiving / spent-return podium

The east loading side receives stronger weathering than the protected front facade.

Use:

- partial threshold/frame damage;
- cracked/worn factory flooring near exposed doors;
- abandoned inbound and spent-return containers at real handoff points;
- limited missing wall/glazing elements around the loading edge;
- one optional D3 service encounter pocket placed off the principal lane.

Keep clean receiving and dirty return distinguishable.

### 6. Harvest / grading / packing / dispatch

The rear loading edge should show interrupted late operations and long exposure.

Use:

- weathered rear opening/canopy elements;
- displaced cardboard/crates/pallets in plausible work zones;
- limited packing-station attrition;
- stronger floor/wall deterioration near the exterior threshold;
- a clear route through the production chain despite clutter.

Do not turn the rear podium into an undifferentiated rubble room.

### 7. Front public/demo and staff-control zone — protected evidence area

This area should be among the least structurally damaged parts of the final ruin.

Requirements:

- retain public/demo glazing and signage in partial but reconstructable form;
- keep the route from the front threshold to the staff-control records area open;
- protect the planned proof chest at `(32, 2, 12)` and its opening volume;
- do not place major collapse, a spawner or mandatory parkour on the proof route;
- allow age/weather detail without erasing the contrast between public optimism and the degraded industrial tower above/behind it.

## Water / weather logic

D3 wet-zone placement must trace actual sources:

- failed greenhouse glazing;
- roof/service penetrations;
- broken upper environmental/water header;
- exterior production-bay glazing loss;
- exposed loading thresholds.

Moss/cracked/aged materials should follow these paths. Do not distribute moss uniformly by random chance.

## Structural retention targets

Gate-C implementation should preserve:

- overall tower silhouette and stepped production mass;
- recognizable public/industrial podium hierarchy;
- at least three clearly distinguishable cultivation floors from exterior/floor-slice review;
- the greenhouse/environmental crown as a damaged but recognizable system;
- principal staff stair continuity;
- west secondary egress identity;
- east freight/service identity;
- Cultivation 04 containment archaeology;
- front deterministic proof route.

## Prohibited damage patterns

Do not use:

- symmetrical missing-wall rectangles repeated floor-to-floor;
- checkerboard/random air deletion;
- giant unexplained blast holes;
- total crown removal;
- total destruction of Cultivation 04;
- identical decay on all cultivation levels;
- rubble filling every aisle;
- damage that buries the proof chest;
- active containment lights/equipment presented as still powered centuries later;
- generic mushroom infestation applied everywhere as a substitute for causal decay.

## Gate-C implementation expectations

Gate C must render at minimum D0, D1 and D3 from the same fixed camera set.

Sanity checks should confirm:

- D1 visible change remains substantially smaller/localized compared with D3;
- D1 changes concentrate on Cultivation 04 containment and real outbound pressure points;
- D3 produces meaningful differences in roof/top, upper-tower and east/rear service views;
- the exterior silhouette remains substantially retained;
- proof-route and circulation assertions survive D3;
- deliberate spawners remain within the 0–2 encounter budget and outside protected routes.

These metrics are regression guards, not substitutes for manual visual review.

## Pass 18 decision

**DAMAGE AND DECAY: COMPLETE FOR GATE-C IMPLEMENTATION.**

OWS-004's final ruin will show a still-recognizable vertical farm degraded from the top/service edges downward by centuries of exposure. The earlier containment intervention survives as a localized historical layer inside that ruin, while the tower's public, production, logistics and proof architecture remain reconstructable.
