# [SYSTEM REPORT] OWS-003 Pass 5 — Scale Translation

**Target:** OWS-003 — Verdant Continuum Foods Cold-Chain Culture Nursery  
**Procedure authority:** `docs/HEAVY_REBUILD_DOCTRINE.md`  
**Status:** COMPLETE FOR GATE-A STUDY

## 1. Scale decision

**Retain the existing 59 x 24 x 51 envelope for Gate A.**

The donor is already large enough to support a credible regional cold-chain/nursery program. Expanding the footprint before proving the massing would create unnecessary site sprawl and weaken the useful adaptive-reuse story.

The retained dimensions should be treated as a **maximum study envelope**, not as a requirement to preserve every existing wall/tree/roof edge.

## 2. Why the envelope is appropriate

At Minecraft's one-block-per-meter abstraction, 59 x 51 provides enough site area for:

- a substantial industrial hall;
- a lower administrative/records annex;
- separate receiving and dispatch thresholds;
- multiple cold-vault/nursery zones;
- conditioned packing/staging;
- refrigeration/environmental plant;
- maintenance access;
- retained orchard/context rows;
- service aprons and setbacks.

The building should feel like a **regional biological logistics node**, not a research campus or distribution megacenter.

## 3. Height hierarchy

The current 24-block total height is acceptable if the vertical composition is rationalized.

### Lower admin / records annex

Target occupied height:

- roughly 7–10 blocks above floor level;
- one principal occupied level;
- roof equipment only where functionally justified.

This preserves a subordinate civic/administrative scale.

### Main cold-vault / nursery hall

Target occupied height:

- roughly 14–18 blocks;
- high enough for tall insulated storage/nursery systems and overhead service trunks;
- visually dominant over the annex.

The hall should remain the hero mass.

### Roof / plant zone

Target:

- selective equipment and screening up to approximately Y21–23;
- separated mechanical masses with maintenance gaps;
- no solid full-roof bunker.

## 4. Structural bay rhythm

Use a primary industrial rhythm of approximately **5–7 blocks**.

This can govern:

- brick/concrete pilasters;
- high hall frames;
- clerestory/skylight organization;
- cold-room modules;
- nursery cell groups;
- loading bays;
- roof plant alignment.

The donor's long roof-light strips should be subdivided or supported by this bay logic rather than reading as floating decorative bands.

## 5. Circulation widths

### Main conditioned operations spine

Target clear width: **3 blocks minimum**.

This route should support staff movement and small material transfer without feeling like a residential corridor.

### Cold-vault / nursery service aisles

Target clear width: **3 blocks** where possible.

Narrow one-block gaps are not acceptable as primary service access.

### Freight receiving / packing routes

Target clear width: **4 blocks** in major handoff lanes, with wider local staging pockets.

### Staff/admin corridors

Target clear width: **2–3 blocks**.

### Maintenance route

Target clear width: **2 blocks** minimum, widening at equipment/service points.

## 6. Door and threshold scale

### Staff/internal controlled doors

- 1–2 blocks wide depending on function;
- use double doors where material carts or cold-chain transfer logically requires them.

### Main operations/cold-room thresholds

- typically 2–3 blocks wide;
- visually heavier/cleaner than ordinary office doors.

### Receiving and dispatch portals

- target 4–5 block clear width;
- complete frame/header/canopy geometry;
- enough vertical clearance to read as real freight thresholds.

The current site should not rely on ordinary door-sized loading openings.

## 7. Cold-vault module scale

A believable storage module should normally be at least:

- **5–7 blocks wide**;
- **5–8 blocks deep**;
- **4–6 blocks interior height**;

with repeated banks or cells and a service aisle.

The exact number of modules should be decided after Gate A establishes the hall's structural rhythm.

## 8. Dormancy nursery cell scale

Individual nursery cells should be large enough to read as controlled biological rooms rather than display cases.

Target internal footprints:

- roughly **5x5 to 7x8 blocks**;
- repeated in a small family of cells;
- observation/service face where useful;
- dedicated environmental-service connection.

At least three visibly distinct nursery/storage compartments should survive to the intact-state design so the building reads as a real system.

## 9. Receiving and quality-hold scale

### Receiving intake/check

Target usable area:

- approximately 7x8 blocks or larger;
- enough for one arriving pallet/crate group, inspection bench and clear through-route.

### Conditioned receiving hold

Target:

- enough space for several inbound loads without becoming the main warehouse.

### Quality hold / seal inspection

Target:

- approximately 6x7 blocks or larger;
- enough for separated suspect stock, inspection bench and replacement seal/repack material;
- should not be a closet.

## 10. Release / licensing / records scale

The release-control function needs a compact but meaningful operating zone.

Target:

- 6x8 to 8x10 blocks across the combined inspection/records relationship;
- observation/control relationship to operations;
- enough wall/desk area for batch routing, destination and release evidence;
- clear player-accessible proof position.

## 11. Conditioned packing and outbound staging scale

### Packing

Target:

- 8x10 blocks or larger;
- two or more parallel work positions;
- side storage for containers/seals;
- clear central transfer aisle.

### Outbound cold staging

Target:

- 8x8 blocks or larger;
- batch/destination rows on the sides;
- direct route to dispatch portal.

## 12. Refrigeration/environmental plant scale

The plant must be visually proportional to the large amount of conditioned space.

Gate-A massing should reserve:

- a roof/side plant field at least roughly 10x12 blocks in total distributed area;
- 3–5 separated equipment masses;
- service walkway/gaps;
- one visible vertical or horizontal service trunk into the hall;
- a dedicated maintenance-access point.

The plant should read as installed equipment, not as a decorative chimney cluster.

## 13. Roof-access scale

Correct the baseline's zero vertical-access-span weakness.

Gate B should ultimately include:

- a real internal/service ladder or stair route;
- a protected landing;
- roof access housing/trapdoor/door;
- clear route to plant equipment.

Gate A only needs to reserve the mass/position for this access so later structure does not have to improvise it.

## 14. Orchard/context scale

The orchard currently occupies a very large share of the site.

For Gate A:

- preserve representative rows and the old agricultural relationship;
- allow selective removal or interruption where service yards, access and building readability require it;
- do not expand orchard coverage;
- do not treat the orchard as the current VCF production hero.

A reduced but coherent orchard can communicate history better than a large green canopy that visually hides the facility.

## 15. Site hardscape

Avoid a single continuous slab.

Use distinct zones for:

- staff/admin arrival;
- inbound receiving apron;
- outbound dispatch apron;
- maintenance/service edge;
- orchard/soft landscape.

This differentiation will help the adaptive-reuse plant read as a working property instead of a building sitting on a generic pad.

## 16. Gate-A scale target

Gate A should therefore study a 59x24x51 composition with:

- retained orchard rows on the west, reduced only as needed for legibility/service access;
- lower front/admin annex;
- dominant central/east cold-chain hall;
- explicit east receiving mass/threshold;
- explicit rear/south dispatch mass/threshold;
- visible roof/side refrigeration plant reservation;
- roof-light/skylight rhythm tied to structural bays;
- planned maintenance-access mass;
- no detailed nursery racks, loot, damage or decorative clutter yet.
