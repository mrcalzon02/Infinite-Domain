## Exterior story

The intact building must visibly tell the story of **adaptive emergency reuse**. It should not look as though VCF designed a new permanent grow factory from the ground up.

Three generations remain readable:

1. older civic/community architecture;
2. the original tall multipurpose hall and daylight lantern;
3. later municipal/VCF emergency conversion elements.

## Retained civic language

- mud-brick / masonry support wing;
- neutral stone public/administrative bar;
- brick high-volume hall;
- repeated civic glazing and pilaster rhythm;
- projecting north lobby;
- roof lantern;
- human-scale public approach.

Exterior repair may refine these elements, but it may not erase them behind continuous white/green cladding.

## North public facade

The north face remains welcoming but controlled:

- original projecting lobby remains centered on the public route;
- restrained emergency weather canopy is tied to the actual doors;
- broad civic glazing remains visible;
- VCF and emergency identity occupy dedicated sign/fascia positions rather than recoloring the entire wall;
- queue markings/barriers remain low and civic rather than military fortifications.

The facade should read as `community center under emergency operations`, not `corporate bunker`.

## Hall exterior

The brick hall remains the strongest solid mass. Its north-facing emergency conversion band may use VCF white/lime materials, but the band must remain subordinate enough that the hall still reads as an adapted civic volume.

The roof lantern remains visually open and must not be screened over by equipment.

## East receiving facade

The east retrofit combines:

- recessed service opening;
- steel side members/header;
- compact weather canopy;
- factory-floor service apron;
- small VCF receiving identity location;
- nearby utility/service cues.

No high-bay dock seal, giant trailer canopy or industrial warehouse facade is appropriate for this neighborhood site.

## South dispatch facade

The south side becomes emergency relief output:

- retained hall exit enlarged/clarified only as needed;
- narrow framed canopy;
- marked relief apron;
- visible staging relationship inside;
- municipal emergency/dispatch signage position.

The dispatch side should look busier and more provisional than the north public face but still structurally deliberate.

## West/service facade

The west wing remains recognizably civic and lower in scale. Existing window rhythm may be selectively retained around staff/support rooms, while the former kitchen/wet-service area receives modest utility vents/pipes and service markings.

Do not transform the whole west wall into exposed machinery.

## Roof composition

- lantern = dominant roof feature;
- grow-service plant = clustered on lower west/rear roof;
- equipment = separated into serviceable modules;
- maintenance gaps = visible;
- east/rear ladder/trapdoor = retained;
- later pipe/vent runs should visibly connect service plant to grow/wet-service functions without covering the roof.

## Material hierarchy

Primary retained building:

- civic masonry / mud brick;
- brick hall;
- smooth stone / polished neutral public surfaces;
- framed glass.

Emergency conversion:

- white / light-gray cleanable surfaces;
- restrained VCF lime;
- localized emergency yellow;
- steel canopy/service frames;
- factory-floor/asphalt only at real service aprons.

## Pass-9 rejection conditions

Gate B must reject the exterior if:

- VCF identity is primarily a green stripe;
- the public entrance loses civic readability;
- receiving/dispatch cannot be distinguished without signs;
- the lantern is visually buried;
- roof equipment becomes a solid bunker;
- emergency canopies dominate the whole building;
- every elevation uses the same material/grammar regardless of function.
