## Exterior findings

1. **Cannery identity dominates.** The visible building is a blunt red-brick shed with copper roof monitors and a chimney-like tower; it does not read as an advanced VCF sanitary packing operation.
2. **The orchard canopy consumes nearly half the site.** Repeated leaf rows and trunks preserve donor origin but do not communicate secure harvest receiving, lot control or food-safe transfer.
3. **Applied branding cannot carry the architecture.** White/lime bands sit on the facade without changing the cannery's massing, thresholds or operational identity.
4. **Public, receiving and dispatch entrances are ambiguous.** Small doors and thin canopies do not establish separate people, raw-product and finished-product flows.
5. **Cold chain is invisible.** No insulated cold-room mass, condenser/evaporator relationship, refrigeration yard or strong dispatch adjacency appears in the silhouette.
6. **Roof composition is inherited rather than operational.** Three long monitors create some industrial rhythm, but roof plant and service connections for sanitation, cooling and ventilation are absent.
7. **Facade depth is weak.** Most wall planes are uninterrupted brick with sparse windows unrelated to internal food-processing zones.
8. **Site logistics are not credible.** There are no distinct truck aprons, dock faces, rejected-lot exit or clean-packaging receiving threshold.

## Interior / sectional findings

The cutaway shows that the functional overlay exists: tanks/pipes, line equipment, cardboard stock, coolers, pallets and a yellow hold area. It also exposes the central defect: those objects occupy a generic donor floor without architectural zoning.

- raw receiving and wet sanitation are not separated by a controlled hygiene transition;
- packing lines do not read as a continuous product path;
- clean packaging stock and finished pallets lack purposeful staging geometry;
- cooler blocks form exposed banks rather than enclosed, insulated cold rooms;
- the yellow rejection field is floor paint around cartons rather than a controlled QA hold;
- offices, staff hygiene/support, quality laboratory/records, maintenance and waste routing are absent or unreadable;
- large volumes remain functionally unexplained.

## Retain / rebuild decision

Retain the long industrial-food orientation, selected roof-monitor rhythm, limited cannery material memory, the western raw-arrival opportunity and the overall envelope for a first massing study.

Rebuild the operational sequence, mass hierarchy, sanitation boundary, cold-chain anatomy, differentiated loading thresholds, staff/public front, roof plant, structural bays and VCF institutional expression.

The goal is not a prettier cannery. It is a credible high-throughput Evercrop harvest, quality, packing and refrigerated-dispatch annex whose normal quality-control systems can later become the setting for the first rejected PT-9 lots.
