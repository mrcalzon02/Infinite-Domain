## Review result

**GATE A PASSED.**

This is approval of the **macro massing and scale only**. It is not approval of the structural system, final facade, interior, operations, corporate signage, historical damage, encounter design, loot, or final D3 schematic.

The r2 composition is strong enough to justify continuing into Pass 7 rather than spending further revisions on shell geometry alone.

## Resolution of the seven r1 blockers

### 1. Front canopy too broad and planar — RESOLVED

The public canopy is now narrowed to the actual entrance zone and supported at its edges. It no longer dominates the entire frontage as a floating slab.

The entrance pavilion itself now creates the arrival hierarchy, which is the correct architectural relationship.

### 2. Central body too visually dominant — RESOLVED FOR MASSING

The former single large central block has been divided into a taller front workplace mass and a lower rear process mass. Their overlap creates a stepped roof condition and makes the side annexes participate in the overall silhouette.

The building remains intentionally rectilinear because this is common neighborhood service infrastructure, but it no longer reads as the grocery donor with attachments.

### 3. Rear canopy insufficiently tied to freight opening — RESOLVED

The rear canopy has been reduced to the actual receiving/dispatch bay and now shares its width/rhythm with the freight opening and service frame.

The rear elevation reads as a service side rather than a second public facade.

### 4. Side/rear structural-service rhythm too weak — RESOLVED FOR MASSING

Large service-bay frames now give both the return side and the clean cold-chain side a visible operational rhythm. A shallow rear records/service bump-out also prevents the freight elevation from becoming one uninterrupted plane.

These are macro frames only. Final columns, lintels, doors, windows and utility penetrations remain Pass 7–10 work.

### 5. Roof equipment too solid and box-like — RESOLVED

The two r1 rooftop prisms have been replaced by four separated equipment masses with open service gaps and a maintenance spine.

The plant now reads as a cluster of serviceable cold-chain equipment rather than rooftop bunkers.

### 6. Mechanical screening too abstract — RESOLVED FOR MASSING

The r1 continuous screen lines have become partial end/side frames associated with the equipment cluster. The plant remains visible and legible instead of being hidden behind a decorative wall.

Final pipework, fans, access and real equipment choices remain an operational-system question.

### 7. Site hardscape too continuous — RESOLVED

The lot now has distinct public arrival hardscape, a building/service walk, a rear freight apron, service strips, and a softer grass perimeter.

The building therefore occupies a neighborhood property rather than floating on a full-lot asphalt diagram.

## Fixed-camera review findings

### Front approaches

- The entrance pavilion is immediately distinguishable from the rest of the building.
- The white/green identity mass is subordinate to the architecture rather than being the architecture.
- The lower return annex and taller cold-chain side generate useful asymmetry.
- The common-site scale remains believable.

### Rear approaches

- The receiving/dispatch mass reads differently from the public pavilion.
- The freight bay has a specific canopy and frame.
- The rear bump-out gives later supervisor/records architecture a credible place without creating a second dominant entrance.

### Roof/top

- The roof now has multiple height zones rather than one forgotten plane.
- The cold-chain plant occupies the correct side/center relationship to the planned refrigerated functions below.
- Equipment spacing is sufficient to support later maintenance-access logic.

### Interior/cutaway capacity

The Gate-A cutaway is intentionally sparse. It confirms only that:

- the public pavilion has adequate depth;
- the west return annex can hold sanitation/inspection and local quality hold;
- the east cold-chain volume can hold clean refrigerated functions;
- the rear process/receiving mass has enough volume for receiving, dispatch and records;
- a staff circulation spine can be developed without expanding the overall bounds.

## Dimension decision

**39 x 13 x 33 is accepted for the next passes.**

The dimension may still be reopened later if the actual structural/interior program exposes a contradiction, but Gate A provides no reason to enlarge it.

## Non-blocking findings carried forward

These are **not** reasons to fail Gate A, but they must be tested later:

1. The broad roof surfaces still need convincing structural rhythm, drainage/service logic and ceiling relationships.
2. Service-bay frames are still diagrammatic and must become real structural/door/utility architecture.
3. The public pavilion needs final facade depth, glazing logic, signage hierarchy and interior alignment.
4. The cold-chain roof plant needs real equipment, utility feeds, access and maintenance clearances.
5. The side/rear elevations need final window/door decisions tied to rooms rather than facade decoration.
6. Overlapping review shells must be rationalized into one coherent structural/interior system before Gate B.

**PASSED — ADVANCE TO PASS 7 STRUCTURAL SYSTEM.**

The next passes may now develop columns/spans/roof support, circulation, facade, interiors, operational systems and full VCF identity. Damage, centuries-of-decay dressing and final D3 ruin work remain prohibited until the intact/operational Gate B is reviewed and passed.
