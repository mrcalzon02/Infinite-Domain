## Baseline interpretation

The current structure reads first as a substantial civic community center and only second as an emergency food-security installation. That distinction is important. The donor has enough architectural competence to be worth converting rather than deleting, but the VCF emergency-grow identity is still an overlay instead of the organizing logic of the building.

The baseline is mechanically healthy: the generated structure is `51 x 21 x 47`, has 13 working doors, zero orphan door halves, 658 window blocks, 99 functional fixtures, a 16-block vertical-access span, and no structural-lint issues. Those facts explain why the donor can support a serious rebuild without implying that its present program is acceptable.

## Visual and spatial findings

### 1. Overall massing and silhouette

**Finding: retain the civic massing, rebuild its emergency hierarchy.**

The broad L-shaped civic wing, taller multipurpose-hall mass and roof lantern produce a useful community-scale silhouette. OWS-002 does not need a larger or more industrial shell simply to look important. Its narrative value comes from seeing ordinary civic infrastructure converted rapidly to food-security duty.

The problem is hierarchy: the silhouette does not yet clearly distinguish public registration, cultivation, service receiving, relief dispatch and mechanical support. The final massing should remain recognizably civic while gaining a much stronger emergency-operational read.

### 2. Front approach and public threshold

**Finding: good donor relationship, conversion incomplete.**

The projecting road-facing lobby is the correct public approach for registration, emergency authorization and culture-kit issue. It should be retained as the primary public threshold.

The current VCF frontage bands do not yet make the intake sequence legible. The final design needs municipal emergency routing, queue control, authorization/issue windows and a visible transition from public lobby to controlled grow operations.

### 3. Multipurpose hall / grow-hall conversion

**Finding: strongest retained donor idea; current overlay insufficient.**

The high-volume hall is exactly the kind of civic space a municipality could commandeer for emergency cultivation. The existing two-tier crop rows correctly establish the concept, but they still read as equipment placed in a hall rather than as an engineered emergency grow system.

The rebuild must give the hall clear crop-service aisles, irrigation distribution, harvest movement, maintenance access, lighting/daylight logic, nutrient support, isolation capacity and a direct operational relationship to packing/dispatch.

### 4. Roof lantern and daylight architecture

**Finding: retain and exploit.**

The glazed hall lantern is an unusually valuable donor feature because it can support both the civic origin and the converted cultivation purpose. It gives OWS-002 a recognizable profile without inventing decorative industrial towers.

Later passes should preserve the lantern unless a specific structural reason requires revision. Irrigation, ventilation or grow-light service may be organized around it, but the roof should not become a random forest of machinery.

### 5. Civic-program baggage

**Finding: rebuild required.**

Library/computer, craft, childcare, generic meeting, auditorium seating and entertainment-stage identities compete with the emergency program. They may leave architectural traces, but they cannot remain dominant active rooms.

The final layout should visibly show a rapid conversion: some civic fabric survives, but room purpose has changed to registration, culture issue, clean stock, nutrient support, grow service, harvest check, packing, records and dispatch.

### 6. Public / staff / freight separation

**Finding: donor circulation is useful but not yet operationally resolved.**

The existing public lobby, independent hall exits, rear service rooms and kitchen give the rebuild enough separate thresholds to establish real flow separation. The current overlay does not yet fully exploit them.

The final program must support two readable paths:

`public arrival -> authorization -> culture/relief issue -> departure`

and

`receiving -> clean stock/nutrients -> grow service -> harvest/wash -> packing -> pallet staging -> outbound dispatch`

These flows may intersect at controlled handoff points, but must not collapse into one generic civic corridor.

### 7. Existing kitchen / wet-service potential

**Finding: retain as converted utility space.**

The community kitchen is valuable because emergency cultivation needs wash, sample-prep, harvest check and relief-food support. Retaining wet-service infrastructure is more believable than inventing a laboratory block elsewhere.

The room should be rewritten operationally rather than cosmetically: wash points, counters, harvest inspection, sanitation and nearby records/packing relationships must explain why the former kitchen survived the conversion.

### 8. Relief dispatch and overflow pressure

**Finding: concept present, architecture weak.**

The existing yellow overflow field and relief stock establish rising demand, but they do not yet form a convincing dispatch lane. The final building needs a physically legible emergency throughput system with crate/pallet staging at real exits or loading points.

Overflow should feel like a strained but organized system, not random supplies placed on yellow flooring.

### 9. Early-containment anomaly

**Finding: preserve restraint, strengthen causality.**

OWS-002 belongs to early containment. The baseline correctly avoids generalized ruin. The eventual anomaly should therefore remain local: one rack, bay or service loop isolated while the rest of the facility continues operating under food-security pressure.

Later D1 work must explain the response spatially through isolation markings, temporary barriers, redirected service or inspection hold. D3 abandonment may add long-term roof, irrigation, glazing and water damage, but must not erase the readable emergency-conversion story.

### 10. Evidence architecture

**Finding: deterministic mechanics protected; placement needs stronger institutional context.**

The proof item `kubejs:emergency_grow_authorization` remains guaranteed through the canonical OWS-002 loot table. The final proof container belongs naturally at registration/authorization or relief-allocation control, where it can demonstrate deliberate civil deployment of Evercrop rather than appearing as generic loot.

## Phase-0 decision

**BASELINE 3D REVIEW: COMPLETE — REBUILD REQUIRED.**

The baseline successfully preserves the historical pre-heavy-rebuild condition and confirms that OWS-002 should be a conversion, not a demolition. The shell and civic massing are worth retaining; the internal institutional program is not.

OWS-002 may proceed to the functional-definition pass under these constraints:

1. the civic origin remains visibly legible;
2. the hall becomes a real cultivation system rather than a room containing crops;
3. public relief flow and back-of-house grow logistics receive separate, controlled circulation;
4. the kitchen/wet-service zone is converted into purposeful harvest/wash support;
5. roof lantern and roof access remain protected unless a documented later gate disproves them;
6. the early-containment anomaly remains local and operationally understandable;
7. deterministic proof acquisition, structure ID, loot identity and quest integration remain mechanically protected;
8. no Gate-A approval is possible until functional definition, precedent research, adjacency and scale translation are complete.
