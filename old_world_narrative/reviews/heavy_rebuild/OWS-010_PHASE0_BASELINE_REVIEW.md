## Overall reading

The baseline reads first as a competent but generic brick-and-gray corporate distribution warehouse with a lower office block, four rear dock portals and two glazed roof monitors. The donor's storage/dispatch anatomy is visibly stronger than a blank box and is useful material for planning.

It does not read as an Atlas conveyor transfer hall. Orange/pink-mapped bands sit on the inherited facades, but the silhouette, roof systems, thresholds and interior process remain warehouse-led. The cutaway shows dense rack and framing lines with a few isolated machine props; it does not show a continuous industrial transfer system.

## Useful donor elements visible in the baseline

- The high-bay warehouse and lower two-level office/support annex create a legible operational hierarchy.
- Four south truck-dock openings and a broad apron establish a real freight edge.
- The north office/public edge and east utility edge remain distinguishable from the truck court.
- Structural piers and clerestory/roof-monitor rhythm give the large hall a bay system that could support transfer lanes.
- The interior cutaway shows a broad cross aisle, repeated storage rows and separated support rooms rather than an empty shell.
- The donor includes quality-control, maintenance/electrical, office/records, staff-exit, refuse and transformer relationships worth studying.
- The common-site scale is sufficient for four lanes without turning OWS-010 into the much larger Atlas factory sites that follow.

## Rebuild-required exterior findings

1. **Wrong first reading.** The building is still a corporate warehouse; Atlas precision, mechanical transfer and maintainability are not expressed in its massing.
2. **Institutional identity is applied.** Orange facade fields do not create full `ATLAS KINETIC INDUSTRIES` identity, transfer-bay hierarchy, standardized lane crowns or purpose-driven signage.
3. **The truck court is generic.** Four dock openings are useful, but inbound/outbound differentiation, transfer staging and lane correspondence are not legible.
4. **No high-bay transfer glyph or dock crowns.** The target's own silhouette contract is absent from the fixed exterior views.
5. **Roof systems are unrelated to process.** Two glazed monitors and one service block do not explain drives, ventilation, maintenance access or material movement below.
6. **Public, operator and service thresholds remain donor-led.** The office entrance reads clearly, but freight, staff, maintenance, parts issue and emergency access are not composed as one Atlas workflow.
7. **No early-anomaly exterior layer.** Nothing visibly identifies a fourth lane being stripped or bypassed to sustain the other three.

## Rebuild-required interior / sectional findings

The cutaway and floor slices confirm the strongest defect: the OWS overlay is thin and mechanically discontinuous.

- Only three depot/press pairs survive serialization; the intended fourth pair is overwritten by a later scaffolding volume.
- No belt, sorter or continuous feed path connects receiving, transfer, inspection, rework and outbound staging.
- Long donor pallet rows still dominate the hall and make the sparse machines read as props placed inside storage aisles.
- Four orange floor strips imply lanes, but they lack lane numbers, shared feeds, guarded service access and distinct input/output ends.
- The scaffolding block is a dense cuboid with no convincingly readable catwalk route, stair, landing or maintenance destination.
- Andesite casings are distributed as small stacks rather than reachable drives connected to a process system.
- Operator circulation and freight circulation are not separated.
- Inspection/calibration, rework, parts issue and lockout stations are not legible as spatial programs.
- The proof chest exists, but its corner placement is not developed into a maintenance office, records cage or canonical proof encounter.

## Historical and narrative implications

The pre-crisis-to-early-anomaly phase is correctly free of generalized destruction. That restraint should remain. The site's unease must come from understandable maintenance decisions:

- a fully legible four-lane normal system;
- one increasingly unreliable lane isolated from production;
- parts and drive casings removed from that lane to sustain the other three;
- manual lockout/bypass and shortened service intervals expressed through work areas, not random debris;
- records architecture that proves Atlas made familiar automation ordinary infrastructure.

The current baseline neither contradicts the phase with excessive ruin nor communicates this causal decline. It is mostly a clean donor warehouse with applied color and disconnected machinery.

## Retain / rebuild disposition

Retain as planning evidence:

- common-site envelope and high-bay/support-annex scale;
- north public/employee, south freight and east service/utility edges;
- four dock openings and rear staging potential;
- structural bay and roof-monitor rhythm;
- cross aisle and support-room adjacency concepts;
- deterministic proof and all integration identities.

Rebuild or reinterpret substantially:

- generic warehouse silhouette and corporate identity;
- facade hierarchy and Atlas signage;
- the entire material-transfer process chain;
- all four transfer lanes and their drive/service anatomy;
- maintenance catwalk/trench access;
- operator/freight circulation separation;
- inspection, rework, parts issue and lockout programs;
- roof/process relationship;
- causal fourth-lane cannibalization story;
- proof-records setting.
