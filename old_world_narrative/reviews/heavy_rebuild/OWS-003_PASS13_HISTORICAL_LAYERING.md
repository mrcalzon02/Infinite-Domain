# [SYSTEM REPORT] OWS-003 Pass 13 — Historical Layering

## D0 — Normal operation

OWS-003 is a successful regional VCF cold-chain nursery operating from a converted orchard cannery. Dormant cultures arrive under batch identity, are condition-checked, pre-cooled or held if necessary, stored in cold vault/nursery cells, released against licensing/routing records, packed under conditioned handling, and dispatched globally.

D0 must read as ordinary biological logistics, not containment:

- receiving operates normally;
- normal quality hold exists for routine shipping exceptions;
- all three dormancy nursery cells remain in service;
- batch registration/licensing records are active administrative infrastructure;
- refrigeration plant and service piping are fully operational;
- outbound staging is controlled but routine.

## D1 — Early anomaly

D1 represents the first repeatable gasket/seal material problem being handled as a logistics and quality-control issue before its wider significance is understood.

Required D1 changes:

1. **One nursery/cold-chain segment becomes suspect, not the whole building.** Nursery 3 is the preferred anomaly focus because it sits close to quality hold and receiving/service infrastructure.
2. **Seal/gasket excursion evidence appears at real interfaces.** Use yellow inspection coding around one controlled threshold, one cooler/service segment and one pipe/riser junction rather than generic contamination paint.
3. **Batch routing changes.** A small quantity of suspect stock is redirected from normal release into quality hold; additional crates/barrels appear at that choke point.
4. **Maintenance attention increases.** One replacement/service station gains spare material, tools or isolated equipment without becoming a disaster scene.
5. **Records imply rerouting.** Licensing/release control remains functional but the physical routing cues show that selected batches are being held or diverted.

D1 must remain localized. Most of the nursery still works.

## D3 — Centuries-later ruin

D3 is not the immediate consequence of the D1 gasket anomaly. It is the same building after abandonment, climate exposure and failure of refrigeration/roof-service systems.

Causal D3 damage priorities:

- roof/service penetrations around the refrigeration field admit water;
- one refrigeration riser/trunk is broken and one equipment group is visibly lost or displaced;
- selected roof-light panels fail, producing localized wet zones below;
- receiving and dispatch thresholds weather more heavily than protected office frontage;
- one cold-room/nursery enclosure loses glazing/panel continuity near the D1 service problem;
- the old orchard becomes irregular/overgrown and partially lost while remaining readable as earlier site history;
- brick shell weathering follows roof edges, openings and wet-service zones rather than random explosions;
- fallen/abandoned pallets and crates accumulate at actual logistics handoffs.

## Continuity requirements across all states

The following must survive D1 and D3:

- front staff entry and route to batch/licensing office;
- deterministic proof route;
- central conditioned operations spine as a readable circulation axis;
- at least one legible cold-vault bank and one dormancy nursery cell;
- receiving and outbound sides remain distinguishable;
- enough refrigeration plant/pipe evidence survives to explain former use;
- VCF and facility identity remains reconstructable;
- OWS-003 Darknet return hook remains reserved and inactive.
