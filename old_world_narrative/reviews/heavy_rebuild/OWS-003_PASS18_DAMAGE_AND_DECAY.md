# [SYSTEM REPORT] OWS-003 Pass 18 — Causal Damage and Decay

**Target:** OWS-003 — Verdant Continuum Foods Cold-Chain Culture Nursery  
**Status:** COMPLETE — planning only until Gate-C implementation

## Damage doctrine

OWS-003 is an Early-anomaly site. The final ruin must not imply that the local D1 gasket problem immediately destroyed the building. D3 represents centuries of abandonment after the wider collapse, with damage concentrated where refrigeration, roof penetrations, glazing and exposed logistics edges would actually fail.

## Causal damage map

### Roof refrigeration field

- remove or displace a limited subset of cooler/plant units;
- break one branch of the roof service header;
- weather the maintenance bridge and one plant-screen edge;
- create small roof openings/failed service penetrations beneath or beside the damaged plant rather than arbitrary craters.

### High-hall roof lights

- fail selected framed-glass panels, especially toward the east/rear service half;
- carry the wet zone downward through localized moss/cracked masonry or damaged interior surfaces;
- retain enough roof-light rhythm that the original cannery conversion remains reconstructable.

### Cold vault and nursery

- keep at least one cold-vault bank substantially intact;
- damage one cooler/service segment associated with the D1 exception area;
- breach a limited part of nursery-3 glazing/paneling rather than destroying all three cells;
- leave transfer frames and operations spine legible.

### Receiving / quality hold

- receiving threshold receives stronger weathering from exposed freight use;
- one door/glazing edge may fail while the loading location remains obvious;
- quality-hold stock becomes scattered/abandoned near its actual decision node;
- optional encounter niche may occupy this degraded service pocket without blocking circulation.

### Packing / outbound / dispatch

- dispatch apron and rear opening weather strongly;
- limited crate/pallet scatter may show interrupted outbound work;
- preserve a traversable route through conditioned packing to the rear threshold.

### Front batch/licensing annex

- comparatively protected and less damaged than roof/service sides;
- proof room remains openable and identifiable;
- no major structural collapse over the deterministic evidence route.

### Orchard/site

- convert neat retained orchard rows into irregular survivors, gaps and overgrowth;
- do not replace the orchard with dense jungle or erase it completely;
- the older agricultural use should remain readable as part of the site’s layered history.

## Prohibited damage patterns

Do not use:

- random checkerboard missing blocks;
- symmetrical ruin holes;
- unexplained blast craters;
- total roof removal;
- total cold-vault destruction;
- damage that destroys every exterior VCF identifier;
- rubble across the proof route;
- active Darknet hardware as final-state decoration.

## Gate-C quantitative expectations

Implementation should use change-density guards as a sanity check, not as automatic approval:

- D1 must be visibly smaller than D3 and remain localized to service/quality-control nodes;
- D3 must be materially different in multiple exterior cameras, especially roof, east/rear receiving and south dispatch views;
- the building silhouette should remain substantially retained and reconstructable;
- protected proof/circulation contracts must survive all damage layers.

## Gate-C review correction

Gate-C r2 preserved a valid D0 and appropriately restrained D1, but fixed-camera review found its D3 exterior too visually pristine for centuries of abandonment. Gate-C r3 therefore freezes D0/D1 and strengthens only causal D3 deterioration at the refrigeration deck, roof-light wet zones, east receiving edge, south dispatch edge and associated service masonry. This remains the same Pass-18 damage doctrine rather than a new historical event.

## Pass 18 decision

**DAMAGE AND DECAY: COMPLETE FOR GATE-C IMPLEMENTATION.**

The final ruin will express refrigeration failure, water ingress, exposed dock weathering and long abandonment while preserving the architectural and narrative evidence needed to reconstruct the site.
