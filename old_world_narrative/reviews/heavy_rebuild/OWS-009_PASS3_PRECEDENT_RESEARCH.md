# OWS-009 Pass 3 — Real Facility Precedent Research

**Target:** OWS-009 — Atlas Roadside Automated Repair Depot  
**Scope:** four functional precedent types; no copied facade or proprietary plan  
**Status:** complete for planning

The target is fictional, so precedent is synthesized from real maintenance-depot, support-core and calibration practice. The lesson is organizational: clear work bays, parallel service support, controlled public access, reliable material issue and documented release testing.

## 1. Tactical equipment maintenance facility repair bays

The U.S. Department of Defense's [UFC 4-214-02 Army Tactical Equipment Maintenance Facilities](https://www.wbdg.org/FFC/DOD/UFC/INACTIVE/ufc_4_214_02_2003.pdf) defines repair bays adjacent to hardstand vehicle access, circulation/scheduled-maintenance bays, full-coverage lifting systems, exhaust evacuation, compressed air, power and wash connections. Its full military scale is inappropriate for a roadside common site, but its anatomy is directly useful.

Translated lessons:

- work bays need clear approach geometry and a readable structural/service frame;
- lifting and service coverage should span the actual work envelope;
- exhaust, compressed air, power and wash/drainage must visibly terminate at each cell;
- machinery requires maintainable side clearance rather than being packed against walls;
- a hardstand/recovery apron is part of the building's function, not decorative paving.

Rejected lessons:

- 192 m² military bays, very high bridge-crane clearances and full drive-through depth would overwhelm this common roadside target;
- combat-equipment scale and redundant support wings are not transferred literally.

## 2. Vehicle operations and maintenance complex

The U.S. Air Force [Vehicle Operations and Vehicle Maintenance Facilities Design Guide](https://www.wbdg.org/FFC/AF/AFDG/ARCHIVES/vehicleoperationsmaintenance.pdf) separates public/customer access from maintenance for safety, uses open repair areas with drive-through lanes and a mechanics' aisle, places paved maneuvering areas at bay entrances, and locates hazardous-waste accumulation toward the rear.

Translated lessons:

- the pedestrian entrance must lead directly to a service desk without entering the bay floor;
- a mechanics/service aisle should run parallel to the bays;
- recovery and outbound maneuvering need deliberate exterior space;
- rework/waste belongs at a rear collection threshold;
- administrative/customer space and industrial work can remain adjacent if access is controlled and observation is intentional.

## 3. Vehicle-maintenance support core

The current U.S. Air Force [Vehicle Maintenance Shop facility standard](https://www.wbdg.org/FFC/AF/AFMAN/214425_Vehicle_Maintenance_Shop.pdf) treats inspection, general repair, major-assembly replacement, testing and cleaning as a coordinated facility, supported by vehicle analysis, tool rooms, parts rooms, hazardous-material/waste rooms, lockers and offices. It also calls attention to vehicle hoists, bay clearances and approach turning geometry.

Translated lessons:

- diagnosis/analysis, repair, testing and cleaning/release are different functions;
- tool control and parts issue are first-order rooms, not leftover shelving;
- removed assemblies and waste cannot share clean replacement stock;
- bay openings, apron width and internal equipment clearance must fit movement, not merely look garage-like;
- the support core can be compacted into one side/rear annex for this smaller site.

## 4. Testing and calibration practice

[ISO/IEC 17025](https://www.iso.org/ISO-IEC-17025-testing-and-calibration-laboratories.html) establishes competence and confidence in testing/calibration results, while the [International Vocabulary of Metrology](https://www.iso.org/sites/JCGM/VIM/JCGM_200e_FILES/MAIN_JCGM_200e/02_e.html) defines metrological traceability through a documented chain of calibrations.

Translated lessons:

- Bay 03 needs controlled calibration references, a repeatable alignment/load datum and release records;
- reference tools belong in a protected calibrator crib, not on the general repair bench;
- inspection results need a custody path from cell to records/service authorization;
- the Atlas service plate and maintenance manual are best housed at the authorization/calibration record node, giving deterministic proof a functional setting.

## Synthesized design rules

1. Keep three compact cells, but assign them distinct roles: diagnosis, exchange/repair and calibration/release.
2. Provide a protected two-block-minimum rear technician/service spine linking every cell to parts, tools and utilities.
3. Give customers a separate front/side service bar with controlled observation.
4. Put parts delivery, tool/calibrator control, rework/core return and waste at the side/rear support edge.
5. Make overhead lifting, exhaust, air and power lines structurally attached and cell-specific.
6. Preserve broad vehicle thresholds and a recovery apron, but avoid military-facility scale.
7. Treat records, calibration traceability and proof custody as spatial program rather than lore decoration.
8. Use Atlas identity to standardize the facility: numbered cells, lockout boundaries, parts issue and line-clear/release markers.

No single precedent is copied. The proposed depot is a compact Minecraft synthesis appropriate to an early common Atlas site.
