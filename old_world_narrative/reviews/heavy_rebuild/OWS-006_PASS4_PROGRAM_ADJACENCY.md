## Program hierarchy

The 59 x 26 x 51 site will be studied as a low-rise research institution with a front public/administrative bar, a central comparative-chamber hall, separated analysis/support wings and a rear service/plant edge. The donor's three-part massing is useful only if those parts acquire clear roles.

## Front / public zone

Required adjacencies:

- forecourt and VCF identity marker;
- reception and visitor orientation;
- small product/research interpretation area presenting PT-9 as beneficial;
- badge-controlled staff vestibule;
- glazed observation gallery facing the comparative chambers;
- public toilets/support only if space remains after required research program;
- no direct public door into an active chamber, preparation room or service corridor.

The front elevation should be cleaner and more transparent than the technical wings while remaining an actual controlled threshold.

## Sample receiving and preparation wing

Required sequence:

`covered service receipt -> accession/check-in -> short-term cold/clean hold -> media/reagent preparation -> inoculation/sample-transfer room -> controlled chamber pass-through`

Support spaces:

- reagent/consumable store;
- wash/sterilization/decontamination point;
- staff handwash/change threshold;
- supervisor/sample-custody workstation;
- waste-return route separated from unopened supplies.

This wing must connect to the chamber service side, not force material through the public observation gallery.

## Comparative chamber hall

The three chambers form one controlled research assembly:

- **A / reference:** EP-7 baseline without PT-9;
- **B / symbiosis:** EP-7 + PT-9 protected culture, visually healthiest and most publicly emphasized;
- **C / stress-recovery:** interrupted nutrient/environmental trial showing paired-system resilience.

Each chamber requires:

- a sealed shell and controlled entry/pass-through;
- one observation face toward the gallery/control side;
- one maintenance/service edge;
- environmental supply and return connection;
- local monitoring/control station;
- internal culture banks rather than a single soil patch;
- enough separation that the three conditions do not visually merge.

The gallery should allow the player to compare A/B/C before entering the deeper analysis wing.

## Bacterial-control and polymer-analysis wing

Coupon preparation and the damaged-material archive should be adjacent but visually distinguished so clean and exposed samples do not become one prop pile.

## Rear service / plant edge

- chamber environmental service manifold;
- water/nutrient/reagent utility support;
- electrical/data control room;
- wash/decon and controlled waste hold;

The roof should visibly continue this system through air-handling units, separated intake/exhaust elements and protected maintenance paths.

## Vertical organization

The 26-block height does not justify a tall tower. Use it to create purposeful section:

- principal research/public floor at ground/lower level;
- partial upper administration/data/observation or mechanical mezzanine where it improves oversight and preserves chamber height;
- chambers with approximately 6–8 blocks of clear internal height for culture banks and environmental distribution;
- roof plant/service layer tied to the rear maintenance core;
- no generic full upper story whose rooms lack functions.

## Exploration route

Primary player sequence:

`public threshold -> visitor interpretation -> comparative observation gallery -> staff/control transition -> preparation/chamber edge -> bacterial controls -> polymer observation -> secure records/proof`

An alternate service loop should expose receiving, utilities, wash/decon and waste handling, then reconnect without turning the facility into a single corridor dungeon.

The proof route must remain traversable after later damage. Optional encounters may pressure intersections or service areas but may not make the only proof destructible or inaccessible.
