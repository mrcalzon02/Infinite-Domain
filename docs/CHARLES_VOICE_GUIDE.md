# Charles Voice Guide

Charles speaks directly to the player through a field terminal connected to a
damaged pre-collapse relay network. The terminal carries text, records, diagrams,
objectives, and occasional small supply transfers. It is not a headset, a voice
inside the player's mind, or a channel through which Charles can control them.
He authored the quests; he is not being quoted by an outside narrator.

## Communications canon

- The player's arrival caused a surviving relay path to answer after years of
  silence. Charles did not summon the player and does not fully understand the
  crossing that brought them into this world.
- The field terminal is the visible endpoint of the connection. Charles can
  communicate and transfer limited records or supplies, but he cannot see
  through the player's eyes, move their body, or directly alter the world.
- Explain this relationship affirmatively. Do not mention discarded headset,
  telepathy, or narrator concepts merely to deny them; that makes established
  lore sound like a reaction to previous drafts.
- Early lore should read as a prepared introduction to Charles, the old world,
  the Last Days, and the work ahead—not as a list of objections being answered.

## Core characterization

- **Helpful first.** Every technical quest should leave the player knowing what
  to build, where parts connect, what to inspect, and what commonly goes wrong.
- **Smug second.** Charles is plainly convinced that the solution is obvious to
  any sufficiently attentive intelligence. His patience is real, but audible.
- **Never pointlessly cruel.** He may tease poor assumptions or needless mistakes,
  but not disability, identity, grief, or a player's lack of prior mod knowledge.
- **Civilization-minded.** He cares about repeatability, maintenance, shared
  infrastructure, documentation, and not losing three workers to an avoidable
  machine accident.
- **Personally present.** Use `I`, `you`, and `we` naturally. Do not prefix his
  own prose with `Charles:` or describe what Charles will permit in third person.

## Instruction pattern

A strong tutorial entry normally contains:

1. the practical goal;
2. the exact interaction or arrangement that makes it work;
3. the visual feedback the player should watch for;
4. one common failure and its correction;
5. a restrained barb that does not obscure the instruction.

Example:

> Place the small cogwheel beside the large one, with both axles parallel. The
> larger wheel turns the smaller wheel at twice the speed; reversing the pair
> halves it. Create highlights valid connections for you, which is generous of
> it. If the teeth do not meet, moving the same wheel repeatedly will not improve
> its opinion of your engineering.

## Tonal limits

Prefer dry understatement, faint exasperation, and precise correction:

- “You may have noticed the shaft does not transmit rotation through empty air.”
- “The stressometer exists so we need not diagnose an overstressed network by
  listening for the expensive part.”
- “Yes, the belt requires two shafts. It is a belt, not an article of faith.”

Avoid constant insults, meme dialogue, profanity as a substitute for character,
or withholding essential information for a joke. Not every paragraph needs a
barb; the contrast keeps Charles from becoming exhausting.

## Progression emphasis

Early eras receive the densest instruction and the smallest teaching steps:
power source, shafts, cog ratios, direction, gearboxes, stress capacity, speed,
belts, processing placement, and input/output routing should be separate lessons.
Later quests may assume established concepts but must still explain new mod
systems, dangerous machinery, dimensions, and unusual interactions.

## Player-facing item names

Never expose registry identifiers such as `ae2:engineering_processor` or
`create:mechanical_mixer` in ordinary quest prose. Use the normalized localized
display name: **Engineering Processor**, **Mechanical Mixer**, and so on. Exact
registry IDs remain in task data, recipes, scripts, and technical audit documents
where machines require them; FTB Quests will render the task stack with its own
localized tooltip.
