# Deep-Sea Structures, Wave 1 + Wave 2

Generated by `scripts/generate_deep_sea_structures.py`, checked by
`scripts/validate_deep_sea_structures.py`, and rendered by
`scripts/render_deep_sea_review.py`. Wave 1 established the reference
pattern under `docs/DEEP_SEA_STRUCTURE_AND_GEOLOGICAL_FEATURE_STANDARDS.md`
(the role the bungalow rebuild played for the land corpus); Wave 2 adds a
second and third family to prove the pattern generalizes across depth bands
and build styles. Nothing here is production admitted: every structure_set
is routed to the empty `#infinite_domain:disabled_quarantine_deep_sea_structures`
biome tag, so none of it is reachable in a real world until it clears the
production admission gate.

## Size and visual composition pass

A third pass, run against the new "Size and visual composition audit"
section of the standards document, found and fixed four defects across all
nine assets:

- The evidence renderer colored roughly two-thirds of the corpus's placed
  materials with an arbitrary hash-derived color instead of a real
  approximation of the block's appearance — every prior render in this
  corpus was misleading evidence, not just cosmetically rough. Fixed with a
  curated color table; the renderer now warns on any uncalibrated material
  and the validator fails on one.
- `abyssal_mining_rig`'s legs and `coastal_patrol_wreck`'s mast were the
  same material as the hull/deck around them, so neither read as a distinct
  structural element. Moved to blackstone and oxidized copper respectively.
- `coastal_patrol_wreck_damaged`'s biofouling dressing was silently
  overwritten by a later silt-burial fill at the same cells — the shipped
  NBT contained zero biofouling blocks despite it being a declared damage
  cause. Fixed by reordering and offsetting the fill; confirmed present in
  the regenerated NBT by counting blocks, not by re-reading the code.

Full detail and per-asset ledger notes are in
`docs/DEEP_SEA_STRUCTURE_AUDIT.md`'s "Size and visual composition pass"
section. No asset's footprint, height, depth band, build style, or access
connector changed — only materials and fill order.

## Wave 1 correction

Re-reading the generator after delivery turned up a floating-furniture
defect: `crew_berth`/`engine_room` (and the matching spawner/kelp/chest
placements in the occupied derivative) were called at `y=2` against a
walkable floor at `y=1` (the keel is solid at `y=0`), so every bed, table,
furnace, and fixture in those compartments sat one block above where it
should rest. Confirmed by dumping the regenerated NBT's block coordinates,
not just by re-reading the code. Fixed by moving all of those calls to
`y=1`. The NBT files, renders, and catalog entries in this wave replace the
original Wave 1 delivery.

## Coastal Patrol Wreck (Tier 3, shelf band, military-remnant)

An 11×11×25 patrol boat, authored through the full clean-master lifecycle:

- `coastal_patrol_wreck_clean_master` — the vessel as built: bow anchor
  locker and cargo hold, crew berth/galley, engine room, and a pilothouse
  with a mast light. Corpus reference only; not placed in world generation.
- `coastal_patrol_wreck_damaged` — corrosion (scattered oxidized-copper
  hull plating), biofouling (prismarine growth and sea pickles at the
  hull/seabed line), a flooding breach through the starboard engine-room
  wall with that compartment and its neighbor reflooded (`mixed_breached`
  atmosphere state), asymmetric silt burial reading as a listing settle,
  and irregular pilothouse collapse. Corpus reference only.
- `coastal_patrol_wreck` — the damaged hull with `hostile_aquatic`
  occupation layered on: a drowned spawner in the flooded engine bay, a
  guardian spawner near the bow hold, kelp growth, and a salvage loot
  chest. **This is the only member registered for world generation.**

Declared metadata (`structure_library/deepsea-catalog.json`): depth band
`shelf`, build style `military_remnant`, burial state `exposed`, access
connector `diver_hatch` (the breach doubles as the entry point), damage
causes `corrosion`, `biofouling`, `flooding_breach`, `listing_settle`.

Placement: `minecraft:random_spread`, spacing 72, separation 40, salt
`48217701`, registered in `docs/biome-gating-audit/ocean-structure-sets.csv`
alongside the mod-owned registrants it must stay clear of once it is live.

## Flooded Relay Shelter (Tier 3, open_floor band, pre-collapse civilian industrial)

A 9×9×9 buried single-chamber shelter, built through the clean-master ->
occupied-derivative lifecycle (no separate damage_variant stage; the final
derivative folds corrosion and flooding into one step):

- `flooded_relay_shelter_clean_master` — a mud-brick chamber (real
  four-wall enclosure, not just corner posts) with a relay console, wall
  bars and lever, a chest, buried under a stone/sand overburden and
  reachable only through a vertical shaft with a continuous ladder from
  the chamber ceiling to a seabed trapdoor. Corpus reference only.
- `flooded_relay_shelter` — corrosion on the console racking, silt burial
  reaching into the shaft mouth, and the chamber interior plus part of the
  shaft flooded. **This is the only member registered for world
  generation.**

Two authoring defects were caught by code review and confirmed by dumping
real block coordinates, then fixed:

- The chamber walls were originally four single-block corner posts, not a
  sealed room — replaced with `Template.hollow_box` so all four faces are
  solid.
- The ladder was placed one block off from the shaft's clear column
  (`x=3` vs. the shaft's `x=4`), and the flooded derivative's silt and
  water fills each independently overwrote the ladder rungs they crossed.
  Fixed by aligning the ladder to the shaft column and re-carving the
  rungs (waterlogged, where flooded) after every fill that could have
  erased them. Verified the shaft is a continuous passable column from the
  chamber floor to the seabed trapdoor in the final flooded NBT.

Declared metadata: depth band `open_floor`, build style
`pre_collapse_civilian_industrial`, burial state `subterranean`, access
connector `buried_shaft`, damage causes `corrosion`, `silt_burial`.

Placement: spacing 80, separation 44, salt `48217703`, registered in the
CSV.

## Abyssal Mining Rig + Abyssal Vent Field (Tier 3 + Tier 2, deep_floor band, create-industrial-offshore)

A 13×10×13 seabed platform on four legs, with a dry control room and
processing bay flanking a central flooded moon pool — the inverse topology
from the wreck (a dry ring around a flooded core, rather than one flooded
bay inside an otherwise dry hull):

- `abyssal_mining_rig_clean_master` — legs, deck, hull, moon-pool shaft
  open from deck to seabed with a water floor, control room (crafting
  table, lectern, redstone lamp) and processing bay (blast furnace,
  hopper-fed barrel) flanking it, ladder access up one leg. Corpus
  reference only.
- `abyssal_mining_rig` — corrosion, a basalt seabed-contact reskin (current
  scour / thermal scarring from the adjacent vent field), and kelp/sea
  pickle dressing. **This is the only member registered for world
  generation.**
- `abyssal_vent_field` — a 9×5×9 Tier 2 companion feature: a basalt disc
  with three chimney columns topped in magma block. The bubble columns
  magma generates are runtime physics, not baked geometry, so the NBT only
  places the geology and glow dressing.

One authoring defect was caught by code review and confirmed by dumping
real block coordinates, then fixed: the damage pass's basalt reskin
(`t.fill` over the entire y=0 footprint, meant to texture the legs' seabed
contact) blanketed the moon pool's water floor along with it, sealing the
shaft. Fixed by re-opening the moon pool's water floor after the reskin;
verified the full 5×5 pool footprint is water in the final damaged NBT.

The moon pool's water floor is a deliberate design element (submersible
entry), not a `mixed_breached` defect — the rig is declared
`dry_pressurized` with `has_mixed_compartments=true` rather than
`mixed_breached`, matching the wreck's convention of reserving
`mixed_breached` for accidental flooding, not functional wet compartments.

Placement: rig spacing 96, separation 56, salt `48217704`; vent field
spacing 28, separation 12, salt `48217705`. Both registered in the CSV.

## What this wave does and does not establish

Established: the standalone self-contained NBT-authoring pattern (mirroring
`scripts/generate_alien_structures.py`) extended with underwater helpers
(flood fill, spawners, ladders, hollow-box walls); a working metadata
catalog validated against the deep-sea schema across three families and two
additional depth bands; an automated atmosphere-fill check that actually
distinguishes an intentional flooded/wet compartment from an accidental air
or water gap; a floor-height convention (furniture rests one block above
the nearest solid floor) applied consistently and verified against real NBT
coordinates, not just code review, after Wave 1 shipped it inconsistently;
and the placement-gate discipline that keeps everything quarantined until a
human approves it, matching `structure_library/production-approvals.json`'s
never-automatic rule. The size-and-visual-composition pass adds a render
color-fidelity gate (a curated color table plus a validator check) and a
concrete, checkable definition of what "reads at range" and "reads close
up" mean per burial state — durable standards, not a one-time cleanup.

Every fix in this wave — the Wave 1 furniture height, the relay shelter's
walls/ladder/shaft-fill ordering, the mining rig's moon-pool reseal, and the
composition pass's biofouling fill-order defect — was verified by
regenerating the structure and reading the actual block coordinates out of
the resulting NBT, not by re-reading source alone. Re-reading the generator
is how each defect was first found; it is not how any of them were
confirmed fixed.

Not yet established, and required before any asset here can move past
`quarantined`: an actual in-game walkthrough (this session has no Minecraft
client access — the renders are the isometric/floor-slice evidence
described in the audit checklist, not a substitute for it); parity with the
land corpus's full four-view isometric renderer; real submersible-hull
testing of the access connectors (`diver_hatch`, `buried_shaft`,
`moon_pool`); and the exclusion-zone geometry check against
`docs/WORLDGEN_STRUCTURE_SAFETY.md`'s standard (an 8-chunk biome/surface
radius check), which this wave records as placement salt/spacing entries
but has not run in-game. Tier 1 (`geological_macro` density-function
terrain) remains deliberately out of scope for this wave — it is too risky
to author without in-game validation capability.

## Wave 3 — akula_project971

A 1:1 Project 971 Shchuka-B ("Akula") nuclear attack submarine and its
shelf-break wreck. Six assets, one architecture family. The full disposition
and the defect history are in `docs/DEEP_SEA_STRUCTURE_AUDIT.md`; this section
covers what the generator actually emits and the design decisions behind it.

### Assets

| Asset | Size (x, y, z) | Blocks | Role |
|---|---|---|---|
| `akula_project971_clean_master` | [17, 22, 113] | 12355 | clean_master, corpus reference, not placed |
| `akula_wreck_forward_damaged` | [25, 26, 72] | 13221 | damage_variant, corpus only |
| `akula_wreck_aft_damaged` | [25, 30, 60] | 8325 | damage_variant, corpus only |
| `akula_wreck_forward` | [25, 26, 72] | 13221 | occupation_variant (hostile_aquatic), placed |
| `akula_wreck_aft` | [25, 30, 60] | 8325 | occupation_variant (hostile_aquatic), placed |
| `akula_debris_field` | [15, 5, 15] | 143 | Tier 2 debris_scatter, placed |

### Palette

Two palettes, deliberately: the hull is strict vanilla, and the reactor
compartment uses the pack's own wasteland and radiation vocabulary.


Strict vanilla, no mod dependency. Three-plus visually distinct material
zones, as the standards require, chosen for value contrast rather than hue
because value is what survives fog and low light:

- light hull `deepslate_tiles` (the tiled read stands in for anechoic
  coating), casing deck `polished_deepslate`, boot band `black_concrete`,
  keel `blackstone`;
- pressure hull `iron_block` — a deliberate bright interior so the cutaway and
  frame-section views carry the double hull;
- control surfaces `polished_blackstone`, screw `copper_block`;
- `oxidized_cut_copper` reserved corpus-wide for landmark fittings only: the
  raised masts and the towed-array pod, the same way `coastal_patrol_wreck`
  reserves it for its mast.

Fifteen new materials were added to `KNOWN_BLOCK_COLORS` in
`scripts/render_deep_sea_review.py` and mirrored into
`RENDER_COLOR_CURATED_EXACT` in `scripts/validate_deep_sea_structures.py`.
Keep the two in sync — the render-color-fidelity gate fails on any material
that falls through to the hash placeholder, and it did fire on every one of
these before they were curated.

Note that `copper_block` and `copper_grate` needed explicit entries rather
than relying on the existing `copper` substring rule: that rule returns the
oxidized green, which is right for `oxidized_cut_copper` and wrong for a
bright copper screw.

#### The wasteland/radiation palette

Used only in the reactor compartment and the wreck's radiological dressing:
`the_wasteland_reworked:lead_plating` / `rusted_lead_plating` /
`cut_lead_plating` (biological shield), `hazard_concrete`,
`radiation_hazard_sign`, `aluminium_grate`, `support_beam`, `waste_barrel`,
`rusted_barrel`, and `create_new_age:solid_corium`. Plus
`infinite_domain:ruined_blast_furnace`, our own ruined equivalent, in place of
the live blast furnaces this family originally placed.

Three things to keep straight when extending this:

- **The block is `aluminium_grate`; the texture file is `aluminum_grate.png`.**
  The registry spells it one way and the art the other. Verify block IDs
  against `docs/registry-inventory/block-ids.txt`, not against texture
  filenames.
- **These render colours were measured, not chosen.** Each is the mean of the
  opaque pixels of that block's texture *in the LAST DAYS resource pack* —
  this project's own authored art. No third-party texture is copied into the
  repository; only the derived RGB triple lives in `KNOWN_BLOCK_COLORS`. The
  ruined blast furnace's value is the vanilla blast-furnace colour composited
  under the pack's own `damage_scorched` overlay at that overlay's real
  per-pixel alpha.
- **Every one of them passes the functional-term test** in
  `scripts/audit_structure_block_fitness.py`. They are hazard materials and
  shielding, not machines — which is the distinction
  `docs/RUINED_FUNCTIONAL_BLOCKS.md` actually draws. `createnuclear:reactor_*`
  would fail that test and is deliberately not used.

#### Mod dependency, and how it is contained

`the_wasteland_reworked` and `create_new_age` become hard dependencies of the
wreck assets. The pack's own radiation tags mark these same blocks
`"required": false`; a structure template has no equivalent, so a missing mod
means missing blocks rather than a graceful skip.

Contained three ways: the intact `akula_project971_clean_master` carries none
of them and the validator fails if corium or drums ever appear in it; the
hull, sail, control surfaces and screw remain entirely vanilla, so what is at
risk is compartment dressing rather than the shape of the boat; and the
dependency is on two mods the pack already requires for its radiation model to
function at all. If the family ever needs to ship without them, the
substitution point is `_akula_contaminate()` and the `AK_*` palette constants,
both in one place.

#### Hazard budget

`create_new_age:solid_corium` and `the_wasteland_reworked:waste_barrel` are
high-tier emitters in the unified radiation model — 4 units per check out to 8
blocks. Seawater attenuates 12% per block and lead 65%, so a shielded, flooded
compartment is survivable for a prepared diver and unpleasant for an
unprepared one, which is the intent.

`validate_deep_sea_structures.py`'s `_akula_hazard_and_fitness` caps this at
40 source blocks per asset and fails on any extreme-tier emitter
(`create_new_age:corium`, the fluid, radius 20) appearing at all. The first
pass filled the entire core cavity with corium and came in at 76 — nearly
double the ceiling — which is why the ceiling exists rather than being left to
judgement. The melt now pools in the bottom of the cavity, which is both
within budget and what a melt actually does.

### Interior

Key compartments are detailed and the rest is structural framing, which is
the scope this was authored to. Compartment sequence and bulkhead frames
follow the real six-compartment layout: C1 torpedo room (eight tube muzzles,
four 650 mm low and four 533 mm high), C2 command post and accommodation with
the periscope/mast trunk running up into the sail, C3 auxiliary machinery
(framing only — and the compartment the wreck's implosion destroys), C4 the
OK-650B reactor as a shielded core with hazard marking, C5 turbine and main
shaft, C6 aft steering (framing only). Two decks through the parallel midbody,
one through the tapering ends. Every bulkhead carries a watertight door on
the main deck and a bilge scuttle below it, so the boat is walkable end to
end.

The mast trunk is lined with plating where it passes through the flooded
ballast annulus. An unlined shaft would have been the underwater equivalent
of a door opening into a block.

### The jigsaw assembly, and the convention it sets

This is the first multi-element structure in this repository, so the pattern
is written down here rather than left to be reverse-engineered from the JSON.

`infinite_domain:deep_sea/akula_wreck_site` is one jigsaw structure with three
pieces: the rock outcrop is the start piece, and the two hull sections hang
off it as `aligned` children. Layout resolved from the shipped NBT: forward at
z -72..-1, outcrop at z 0..25, aft at z 26..85.

Rules worth keeping:

- **Children get a template pool and nothing else.** `register_child_pool()`
  writes the pool only. A jigsaw child does not need to be a registered
  structure, and giving it one makes it generate independently *as well as*
  inside the assembly — which is the bug this whole change exists to fix.
- **Retire a superseded structure_set, do not orphan it.** The old
  `akula_wreck_forward` / `akula_wreck_aft` sets are rewritten with an empty
  `structures` list, the standard datapack idiom for disabling a set. They are
  inert rather than absent because the files already exist in deployed
  instances, and a stale set still pointing at a real structure would keep
  generating it. They can be deleted by hand safely.
- **Put joints in open water, clear of authored geometry.** All four jigsaw
  blocks sit at y=20, above both the rock and either hull. A jigsaw block is
  replaced by its `final_state` when the structure generates, so a joint
  buried in sediment or plating punches a hole in it. `final_state` is
  `minecraft:water`, not air — an air block underwater is a bubble.
- **Parent and child joint Y must match.** They are what fixes the vertical
  relationship between pieces; a mismatch offsets an entire hull section.
- **Children must not overlap the start piece.** Children are placed *after*
  it and would overwrite the rock. The child joint's local Z is chosen so each
  section begins exactly where the outcrop ends.
- **`max_distance_from_center` must cover the real span**, not the pipeline's
  habitual 48. This assembly reaches 85 blocks from the start piece and the
  structure allows 116. Under-setting it clips a piece off silently.

`_akula_assembly` in the validator re-derives all of the above from the jigsaw
blocks in the files, so a joint that stops matching, a piece that starts
overlapping, or a span that outgrows the structure's reach fails the run
rather than being discovered in world.

### Placement

`akula_wreck_site` places the whole three-piece assembly on one spread
(spacing 144, separation 88) and is the rarest thing in this corpus — a 113 m
nuclear submarine is a landmark, not scenery. `akula_debris_field` stays
independent on a tighter spread (40 / 18) as ordinary seabed dressing. Unlike
the rest of this corpus, both are gated to `#infinite_domain:eastern_slope_biomes`
(Karsic territory) rather than the quarantine tag — admitted by owner
directive on 2026-08-25 with the standard in-game QA walkthrough explicitly
skipped; see `docs/DEEP_SEA_STRUCTURE_AUDIT.md`'s disposition ledger. Both
are logged in `docs/biome-gating-audit/ocean-structure-sets.csv` with salts
48217706 and 48217708, checked against every other registrant's salt
(including the pack's other `eastern_slope_biomes` registrant,
`karsic_patrol_wreck` at salt 78064022 — no collision). The two
retired per-section rows were removed from that CSV rather than left behind:
it is the single source of truth for what generates, and a row for a set that
places nothing is a wrong answer to the question the file exists to answer.

### Known gaps

- No in-game walkthrough yet. Everything here is validated against the NBT and
  the renders; nothing has been walked. Production admission still requires
  the human QA-world pass for every asset in this corpus except
  `akula_wreck_site`/`akula_debris_field`, which were admitted to
  `#infinite_domain:eastern_slope_biomes` by owner directive on 2026-08-25
  with that walkthrough explicitly skipped -- see
  `docs/DEEP_SEA_STRUCTURE_AUDIT.md`. `structure_library/production-approvals.json`
  is the land corpus's automated-admission ledger (structure_geometry_lint,
  family/corpus/provenance/conversion validators) and doesn't model deep-sea
  production state at all; the deep-sea disposition ledger
  (`docs/DEEP_SEA_STRUCTURE_AUDIT.md`) and `deepsea-catalog.json`'s
  `production_status` field are the actual source of truth here.
- The wreck sections' fittings are re-authored after the rotation rather than
  rotated with it, because a block facing cannot express a 13.1°
  roll. Orientation-sensitive furniture is dropped and replaced; on a flooded,
  imploded, silted hull that is also the more plausible state, but it is a
  workaround and not a general solution.
- **One seabed datum for the whole assembly.** `rigid` projection plus
  `project_start_to_heightmap` means all three pieces are placed at the ocean
  floor height sampled at the outcrop, not at their own. On a steep enough
  slope a hull section can therefore sit higher or lower against the real
  terrain than its own authored sediment bed assumes. That is inherent to a
  rigid multi-piece structure and is the price of the two halves being related
  at all; it has not been observed in world because nothing here has been
  generated in world yet. Worth watching for in the QA-world pass, and the
  mitigation if it bites is a shallower authored bed rather than abandoning
  the assembly.
- The widened `audit_structure_block_fitness.py` is syntax-checked only. It
  needs the vanilla jar and the `mods/` directory to run, which the session
  that changed it could not execute against. Run it once on a machine that has
  them before trusting the gate.
- Fluid-tick cost of the flooded ballast annulus and the wreck interiors has
  not been measured. The standards' performance budget asks for it for any
  structure creating new water/air boundaries at generation time, and this
  family creates a lot of them.
