# Infinite Domain — Post-Calamity Event Structures Plan

Status: **planned / required / not yet fully authored**

Role: **narrative worldgen, final-conflict environmental storytelling, exploration support, calamity visualization, Spore integration, Lost Cities roadway integration, and Heavy Rebuild target family**

This document is the planning authority for structures that depict the **terminal collapse and immediate post-calamity aftermath** of the Old World. These sites are not generic ruins. Their purpose is to show how civilization failed during the final fungal-spore crisis: evacuation, containment, mass casualty handling, infrastructure failure, emergency military action, panic, abandonment, fire, collision, biological overrun, and the conversion of the dead environment into biomass and sporematter.

The required visual progression is:

**functioning civilization -> emergency response -> final conflict / evacuation -> containment failure -> fungal overrun -> post-calamity wasteland**

The structures in this program occupy the missing middle between ordinary Old World ruins and the fully established Wasteland.

---

## Core design doctrine

Every structure in this family must communicate a specific failed human activity rather than merely being "a ruined building with fungus on it." A refugee camp must still read as a refugee camp. A farm must still communicate food production. A highway must show evacuation and traffic collapse. A burial site must show organized disposal giving way to biological overrun.

Spore content should therefore be layered **onto coherent purpose-built structures** rather than used as a substitute for structural design.

The Spore mod is the primary biological-overrun vocabulary for this program. Biomass, fungal material, infestation, contaminated interiors, breach points, and organic reclamation should appear in escalating amounts appropriate to each site's chronology and damage state.

All large structures produced under this plan should eventually receive Heavy Rebuild treatment, including purposeful interiors, logical access, multiple damage states where appropriate, loot/evidence placement, hostile encounter design, and multi-angle visual review before promotion to final-quality worldgen.

# Required blueprint families

## PCE-001 — Fungal-Choked Catastrophe Craters

Large cratered sites representing impact, bombardment, containment detonation, fuel/ammunition secondary explosions, industrial failure, or other final-conflict destruction.

These sites should not resemble ordinary terrain depressions. Required visual language includes:

- collapsed and thrown structural debris;
- scorched or vitrified ground where appropriate;
- torn road, utility, or industrial fragments;
- fungal veining descending into the crater;
- dense biomass mats and sporematter accumulation;
- partial structures leaning or collapsed toward the crater;
- contaminated water or drainage where mechanically practical;
- optional evidence suggesting what caused the event without requiring every crater to share one explanation.

Variants should range from recently devastated sites with surviving recognizable infrastructure to late-stage craters almost completely converted into fungal biomass basins.

## PCE-002 — Overrun Refugee Camps

Temporary civilian survival and evacuation camps that failed during the final days.

Required components should include combinations of:

- tents and improvised shelters;
- barricades, fencing, traffic barriers, and controlled entrances;
- triage and emergency medical areas;
- ration and aid distribution points;
- water storage and field kitchens;
- cots and temporary habitation zones;
- abandoned personal effects;
- emergency generators and lighting remnants;
- failed defensive positions;
- hurried evacuation evidence;
- body collection or disposal zones;
- Spore infestation ranging from isolated breach points to total biomass overrun.

Several chronological/damage variants are desired: orderly emergency camp, overcrowded failing camp, active final-defense camp, recently overrun camp, and heavily reclaimed biomass camp.

## PCE-003 — Lost Cities Intercity Highway Collapse Sections

A dedicated family of road, overpass, interchange, tunnel, and highway assets showing mass evacuation and transport-system failure.

These should be designed for compatibility with or adjacency to Lost Cities road systems where practical.

Required scene language includes:

- wrecked civilian vehicles;
- buses and evacuation transport;
- freight vehicles and jackknifed cargo;
- emergency and service vehicles;
- military or police roadblocks;
- stalled traffic queues;
- multilane collisions;
- abandoned luggage and supplies;
- damaged barriers and signs;
- ruptured road surfaces;
- burned convoy sections;
- fungal biomass growing through vehicles and pavement;
- collapsed overpass or bridge variants;
- crater-damaged highway variants;
- checkpoint and quarantine-corridor variants.

This family should make long-distance travel corridors themselves part of the catastrophe narrative rather than treating roads as empty connectors between structures.

## PCE-004 — Destroyed Urban and Suburban Calamity Blocks

Purpose-authored city and neighborhood destruction that clearly belongs to the final conflict rather than generic age-related decay.

Candidate structures include:

- collapsed apartment buildings;
- shelled office blocks;
- burned civic buildings;
- destroyed emergency-services facilities;
- shattered hospitals and clinics;
- partially collapsed commercial strips;
- residential streets with barricaded homes;
- evacuation staging buildings;
- ruined schools or community shelters;
- utility failures and burst infrastructure.

Spore intrusion should exploit real structural pathways: basements, drainage, broken walls, ventilation routes, rooftops, service tunnels, collapsed floors, and flooded lower levels.

## PCE-005 — Burned Farmsteads and Rural Collapse Sites

Rural structures documenting failed food production, quarantine burning, abandonment, and biological spread beyond cities.

Required blueprint candidates include:

- burned farmhouses;
- collapsed barns;
- machine sheds;
- grain silos;
- livestock enclosures;
- irrigation equipment;
- contaminated wells;
- field-edge storage;
- improvised rural quarantine checkpoints;
- agricultural evacuation scenes.

Environmental evidence should include dead or abandoned fields, firebreaks, attempted controlled burns, fungal crop takeover, damaged equipment, contaminated livestock areas, and biomass spreading through barns or storage buildings.

These sites are important because they demonstrate that the calamity destroyed the food-production base as well as population centers.

## PCE-006 — Mass Grave Pits and Biomass Disposal Trenches

Large-scale emergency body-disposal locations that were subsequently overwhelmed or biologically reclaimed.

These are major calamity indicators and should be visually severe without relying on gratuitous detail.

Required variants include:

- organized trench burial sites;
- military quarantine burial zones;
- failed cremation areas;
- bulldozed emergency disposal pits;
- hospital or refugee-camp overflow burial areas;
- improvised civilian mass-disposal sites;
- later-stage pits almost entirely converted into biomass and sporematter reservoirs.

The defining feature is **scale**. These sites should communicate that institutions were processing mass casualties and eventually lost control. Biomass and Spore matter should be exceptionally dense here, making these sites plausible high-risk fungal ecosystems and potential lore/evidence locations.

## PCE-007 — Calamity Indicator Microstructure Library

A reusable scatter-library of smaller assets that can reinforce the terminal-collapse story around roads, cities, farms, camps, industrial locations, and wilderness edges.

Required candidates include:

- emergency roadblock remnants;
- quarantine fencing;
- warning checkpoints;
- burned vehicle pull-offs;
- abandoned supply drops;
- small triage stations;
- collapsed observation/watch posts;
- barricaded homes;
- biohazard disposal skips;
- fungal breach points in walls and tunnels;
- civilian evacuation signage;
- failed aid stations;
- contaminated drainage outlets;
- abandoned generator points;
- destroyed communication relays;
- emergency fuel caches;
- wrecked ambulances or service vehicles;
- partial military field positions.

These should be suitable as independent small structures or adjunct pieces placed around larger sites.

# Spore integration doctrine

Spore-mod content should be intentionally tiered by site state rather than applied at random.

### Stage A — Early contamination

Human function remains dominant. Spore presence is localized to drains, basements, corpse-storage areas, cracked containment, contaminated cargo, or isolated organic growth.

### Stage B — Active failure

Fungal growth has breached occupied spaces. Barricades, fire damage, abandoned equipment, emergency response remnants, and combat/containment evidence coexist with expanding biomass.

### Stage C — Overrun

The structure remains recognizable, but human control is gone. Spore growth occupies major rooms, streets, vehicles, trenches, or production spaces.

### Stage D — Biomass reclamation

The former structure is partially submerged beneath fungal matter. Architecture survives mainly as a readable skeleton explaining what the location once was.

Not every structure requires all four versions, but major blueprint families should eventually contain multiple chronological or damage-state variants.

# Loot, encounter, and narrative rules

Post-calamity structures are primarily **evidence and exploration locations**, not free progression caches.

Appropriate rewards include:

- Old World records;
- emergency logs;
- evacuation documents;
- biological samples;
- fungal research material;
- industrial salvage;
- damaged equipment;
- chemical precursors;
- ration remnants;
- identification material;
- quest evidence;
- faction/corporate clues;
- limited ammunition or survival supplies where already progression-compatible.

Do not use these sites as convenient sources of intact advanced production machines or materials that bypass established era progression.

Hostile encounters should use Spore creatures and other appropriate enemies according to site state. Large biomass pits, grave zones, deep fungal craters, and fully overrun camps may support much heavier Spore presence than ordinary ruined structures.

---

# Relationship to existing Old World narrative

This program should reinforce the established catastrophe narrative in which fungal and bacterial systems escaped meaningful containment and institutions continued attempting responses that were ultimately incapable of stopping the spread.

Post-calamity structures should provide physical evidence for:

- early scientific warnings;
- failed containment;
- emergency government/corporate response;
- food and logistics collapse;
- mass evacuation;
- transportation failure;
- casualty accumulation;
- increasingly desperate containment tactics;
- final organized resistance;
- ultimate biological overrun.

These locations should be added to the existing Exploration quest framework where relevant rather than replacing the existing exploration chain.

---

# Lost Cities and worldgen relationship

Lost Cities should provide broad urban/road context where appropriate, while Infinite Domain-authored structures provide the **specific narrative scenes** that generic procedural ruins cannot reliably communicate.

Highway collapse pieces should be designed so that they can plausibly join, replace, interrupt, or sit beside Lost Cities road segments without requiring the entire road system to be hand-authored.

Large catastrophe structures should remain uncommon enough that each retains narrative impact. Microstructures may be substantially more common and can be used to gradually increase calamity evidence as players move through heavily affected regions.

---

# Heavy Rebuild requirements

Any rough schematic created for this program is an implementation scaffold, not final visual acceptance.

Final promotion should require the established Heavy Rebuild doctrine, including:

- real-world purpose reference;
- clear functional layout;
- believable scale under Minecraft block constraints;
- detailed interiors where accessible;
- damage that follows structural logic;
- purposeful Spore overgrowth;
- encounter and loot placement;
- narrative evidence placement;
- multi-angle preview inspection;
- multiple damage states where the blueprint family benefits from them.

This order establishes the most broadly reusable environmental vocabulary first and then builds toward rarer high-impact catastrophe locations.

## Permanent program objective

When this program is complete, a player exploring the Wasteland should be able to reconstruct the final months, weeks, and days of the Old World simply by reading the environment: traffic that never escaped, camps that were overrun, farms burned in desperate containment attempts, cities damaged during final conflict, graves turned into fungal biomass reservoirs, and infrastructure gradually disappearing beneath Spore growth.

The calamity should no longer exist only in books and quest text. **The world itself must show how civilization died.**
